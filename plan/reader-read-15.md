### 4.1 The items, routed

**The listing as a list of facts.** Direction gap, with our own text as the cause — the §116
shape. The brief this listing was drawn from (`runs/pilots/pilot19/brief.txt`) is itself a
comma-separated list of facts, and §136 measured that the brief outweighs every rule in the
listing prompt; the listing mirrored the brief's shape faithfully. The listing task's eighteen
demands are prohibitions about plainness and presence; none names a shape, and the summit
blurbs on disk all have one (a situation, the turn, the promise). Fix at the brief first, which
costs nothing, before any clause.

**"which is a thing a grown man can be bad at, and I was."** No rule names this and none of the
reviser's thirteen prohibitions reaches it. It is one instance of the family §4.2 puts first: the
paragraph that ends on a turned, weighted line. Gap — and the family is the model's, not the
world's.

**"back room nobody bowls in."** Enforcement defect, third sighting after reads 11 to 13. §179's
clause — *a clause naming an absence or a permission nothing had put in question* — has been on
the reviser's instruction since §187, this chapter went through the reviser, and the absence
survived beside eight more of its kind in the two chapters. §187's own finding stands: no clause
has ever moved this axis.

### 4.2 The coordinator's own read (solicited; a diagnostic, never evidence)

I read *The Primal Hunter* and *Defiance of the Fall* chapter 1 in full, then ours again. The
operator is right, and the reason is not that ours are worse sentences. Sentence for sentence
ours are cleaner: *Primal Hunter* opens on the accursed alarm and the sweet embrace of the
blankets, drifts through a shower, traffic and a receptionist for a thousand words, tells us
Jake is laid-back and a loner in so many words, and only reaches the system in its last line.
That is the shape of the real deal, and it reads as a person because the narrator is
unguarded: *Maybe someone brought donuts?* — *No donuts, he noted internally with great
disappointment* — *her cheating on me with my best friend, does count as "not panning out",
right?* Zac in *Defiance* squeaks, screams, pinches himself, slaps himself, rages at a rigged
roll and says *Phew.* Both narrators feel in plain words and out loud, and the system talks
back to them in boxes with a personality of its own.

Ours perform restraint. Every paragraph is composed, and a reader of this shelf has read a
million words of unguarded narration and recognises composure as the machine's signature. The
mechanisms, in the order of how loud they are on the page:

1. **The turned last line.** Nearly every paragraph closes on a wry, weighted clause:
   *which is a thing a grown man can be bad at, and I was* — *because the alternative was
   Sunday* — *and about myself I could not tell you one useful thing* — *That is the whole of
   it; that is the only thing I have ever had* — *That's the part I can't explain to people* —
   *It had been laid maybe four minutes, and it was doing work already* — *and I put that away
   to think about later*. *Primal Hunter*'s paragraphs simply stop. One shape every sixty words
   is the loudest tell in both chapters, no clause names it, and I do not believe a clause
   would: it is the model's idea of a good paragraph.
2. **The narrator announcing his own moves.** *Here is what I was good at.* — *I want to be
   honest about this.* — *Here is the thing I learned in that room, which I would like written
   down somewhere in case I am wrong about it later.* — and *That is the part I keep coming
   back to*, verbatim in both chapters from two writers. Scaffolding a person telling a story
   never builds.
3. **Definition by what a thing is not.** *nobody bowls in* — *nobody plays any more* — *Not
   shook. Went.* — *no tell, no seam, no shadow, no draught* — *Nothing hurt, and nothing had
   touched me* — *did not blink, and it did not go away* — *Nothing hurried me, and nothing on
   the sheet went away* — *nobody had scratched them*. Nine in two chapters, all through a
   reviser whose instruction forbids exactly this.
4. **The same simile shape, nine times.** *the way you know a face* — *the way you hear things
   through a fan* — *the way you feel a draught* — *the way you go through a step that is not
   there* — *the way a mug is warm ten minutes after* — *the way dust lies on a shelf* — *the way
   a woman checks an oven door* — *the way a measurement gets read out*. Each one completes
   without stopping, so each passes §176's clause, which was written for the comparison that
   fails and not for the tic.
5. **Feeling reported as its absence or as an index, never named, never loud.** *By then I
   wasn't afraid* — *My hands were shaking so hard I could hear them on the stone* — *my chest
   was a bag of broken glass*. Nobody in either chapter exclaims. A nineteen-year-old dropped
   through a lecture-hall floor into a dungeon narrates like a spy novel; the readership the
   product names wants Zac's *SHIT!* and Jake's donut. This is the deepest cause and it is
   ours: the floor's clarity, the iceberg, no restatement, no narrating the inference, and the
   reviser's rules all point at composure. **Our narrator is a stylist. The genre's narrator
   is a fan.**
6. **The system has no voice.** *Primal Hunter* ends on the system's own line; *Defiance* is
   half system boxes, each with a manner (*[ERROR! Herald occupying same space as you!
   Adjusting…]*), and the roll screens are reproduced as objects. Ours print one `[STATUS]` row
   a scene and narrate everything else the system does (*the Judge wiped my sheet clean. Three
   words came up on it*). §161's furniture contract prints one parsed line exactly once; the
   genre's system is a character with lines, and nothing in the pipeline gives it any.
7. **Proof of seeing.** *the little display on the microwave in the crew room* — *Carpet and
   chipboard and the cold ache of the radiator pipe* — *a warm can going flat on the ledge* —
   sodium light twice. *Primal Hunter*'s office is *a massive monstrosity of glass*. The reader
   did not come to watch the prose notice things.

What is good in ours, and it is real: the shape. Both chapters open on the life before, arrive
at the system inside it, read the sheet aloud as business, use the exception on the page, move
a number where the reader can see it, and end on an offer unanswered. The census confirms it
and the operator did not dispute it. The shape is right and the voice is wrong, and the voice
is the thing fifteen reads have named from fifteen directions.

### 4.3 What this read settles about the method

Every register clause this project has written targets one shape, and the record (§187, and
now items 3 and 4 above) shows the model keeping the register and moving to the next shape. The
two things never tried are the two that would change what the model is aiming at rather than
what it is forbidden: showing the writer the target register, which RS1 forbids and is the
operator's to reverse; and writing the narrator's stance into who the writer is, which the
roster's own rail against craft words in a dossier makes narrow but not impossible. A third,
cheaper than either, is the control this project respects everywhere else: an arm with the
house floor off, to measure how much of the composure is ours.
