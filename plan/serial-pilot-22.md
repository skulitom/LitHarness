# Serial Pilot 22 — the first book drawn under a concept

**Status: READ at draw2, 2026-09-02; the operator's milestone.** The first draw under stage-0 §197's concept stage, for the
loop the operator set on 2026-09-02 (`plan/serial-pilot-21.md` §5): the coordinator hand-reads
each draw on both halves, routes its defects, makes the structural fix, redraws. This pilot
answers draw 4's read (`serial-pilot-21.md` §5.4): no horizon a reader could feel, the listing
at fault on scale, and nothing above the listing in the pipeline.

## 0. What this is, and what it is not

One writer (`carver`, the far-listener stance, §5.4) invents the book before its listing; the
listing is written from the concept; the seed is told what the world has to be able to hold;
the outline plans the first arc against it; its debts open the promise ledger before scene
one. Same shelf (three exemplars), same strip, reviser off, first person. **n is one.** The
coordinator's read is a diagnostic and never evidence (§95); two draws are two draws
(`serial-pilot-15b.md` §0). Nothing here ranks: the concept was drawn once and is the book.

## 1. The concept, as drawn

Empty brief, first person asked as a position, the three exemplars' listings shown above the
brief, `--scenes 6` (the pilot's book: three chapters of two scenes). One call, no readers, no
title. Settled at `runs/pilots/pilot22/concept.json` (gitignored), and recorded here verbatim
because it is the thing this pilot is about:

> Who they were the day before: He was a night-shift parcel sorter at a regional delivery
> depot, one resit short of finishing a sports-science degree.
>
> What they alone have, from the first chapter: He is the only undeliverable address Dispatch
> has: every skill, class and rung the System fails to hand over — because the person it was
> issued to died before it landed — routes to him as a parcel nobody else can see, hear or sign
> for, and it does this from the first shift after integration.
>
> What they want past the next step up: To reach rung 3, the lowest rung Dispatch issues a
> livery for, before the depot's assigned cohort gets there.
>
> The system, Dispatch. How it shows itself: It speaks only in delivery notices — tracking
> numbers, 'Attempt 1 of 3', consignment weights, polite regret at a failed handover — never in
> praise, threat or explanation, and it never uses his name, only his address.
>
> How far up it goes: 9 steps. Where the strongest person anyone has heard of stands: Rung 5 of
> 9, held by a man in Lagos the notices call Consignee 0001; nobody alive has heard of a 6.
>
> The turn, inside the first arc: A parcel arrives with a return address from a world that is
> not theirs — a world integrated and finished long before Earth's — and signing for it enrols
> him in that dead world's system, which was never switched off and is still executing its
> last instruction.
>
> A second system after the turn, the Standing Order. How it shows itself: Undated imperative
> work orders addressed to no one, issued in a dead world's shift language, stamped with a
> completion date that has already passed.
>
> What carries over from the first: His address — he is still the only valid recipient, so
> Dispatch's parcels keep arriving inside the Standing Order's jurisdiction — but not his
> rung: the Standing Order counts him from zero and counts in liveries, not numbers.
>
> The first arc opens: Integration lands mid-shift on the depot floor; every sorter on the
> belt gets a class notification and he gets a failed-delivery notice instead.
>
> Its middle: He signs for his first undeliverable — a combat skill issued to a man who died
> on the floor above during the same minute — and uses it in front of six witnesses, so the
> depot now knows he holds something the System did not give him.
>
> It closes: Attempt 2 of 3 arrives with a return address from a world that isn't theirs; he
> signs for it on the loading dock, and the Standing Order's first work order prints in a hand
> nobody at the depot can read.
>
> What the book owes, and the scene each is due by:
> - The cost of holding a dead man's skill: What the System charges a recipient for carrying
>   goods issued to somebody else, shown on his body, not stated in a notice. (by scene 3)
> - Why the address is him: The reason Dispatch's undeliverables route to a night sorter and
>   not to the depot's assigned cohort — a fact about the depot itself, not about him being
>   special. (by scene 4)
> - Priya Rademaker, the shift supervisor who drew a real class at rung 2: Whether an issued
>   class beats a salvaged one when they meet, settled by contact, not by argument. (by
>   scene 5)
> - Attempt 3 of 3: What Dispatch does with a consignment that goes unsigned, demonstrated on
>   something he can't afford to lose. (by scene 6)

**What the coordinator sees in it, before anything is drawn from it.** The invention is the
stage's own and nothing in the prompt names any of it: a System that speaks only in delivery
notices and addresses him by his address; the undeliverable address as the exception; a dead
world's system still executing its last instruction as the turn, with the address carried
over and the rung not, which is the shape the operator's own example has (a competing system,
some of the old kept); a horizon as a count (nine steps, the strongest known at five, nobody
at six); four debts due by scenes three to six, the first of them the price of the hook. The
turn lands at the arc's close (scene 6), past this pilot's chapter 1 (scenes 1 and 2), so the
sheet swap §197 refused is not needed for this draw. One thing to watch at the read: the
exception is a keep-power handed at the top (`overview._system`'s docstring measured that
shape in seven of eight listings under the accumulation clause), and the debt due by scene 3
is what prices it; whether it reads as handed or as earned is the read's to say.

The concept's own words carry two of this house's machinery words (*rung*, *rungs*); the
listing loop's redraw rail (`schema_words.named_in`) reads them only where they are used as a
name, and the seed is not reader-facing. Counted, not acted on.

## 3. The listing

**Drawn 2026-09-02 from the concept**, one draw and no redraw fired (no machinery word used
as a name; coordinator density under the ceiling); titled *Failed Delivery Notice*, free on

> I sorted parcels on the night shift at a delivery depot. The System arrived mid shift, and every sorter on the belt got a class. I got a failed delivery notice. Dispatch talks only in parcels, tracking numbers, polite regret, and it never once uses my name. **What the coordinator sees in it.** It is a story and not a list of facts, which was the
operator's first item on pilots 19 and 20 (`plan/reader-read-15.md`): the concept did the
inventing and the listing only had to tell it. The person, the hook, the system's manner, a
first payment already made (*read a dead man's combat skill sitting there as mine*) and a
want with a clock on it (*before the rest of my depot gets there*) are all on the page in
115 words. The horizon is on the page as a ladder to climb (*far enough up Dispatch's
ranks to be given its colours*) and not as the count the concept holds (nine, the strongest
known at five, nobody at six), which is the market's own habit in a listing; the seed holds
the count. Counted, not acted on: *nobody else can see or sign for* and *never once uses my
name* are the absence family twice, both earned by the premise. The turn is not in the
listing at all, and should not be: it lands at the arc's close.

**The engagement half, on the fixed questions.**

**Three findings, and their routing.**

1. **The system was not live, and the chapter did not need it to be good** (§4 above). The

3. **The horizon's count stays in canon.** The concept holds it and the seed declared it; the
