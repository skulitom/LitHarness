# Serial Pilot 24 — a power that works in chapter one, in both persons

**Status: RUNNING, 2026-09-02.** The first concept drawn under stage-0 §198's four fields (a
first use, a threat, a prize, a look), answering read 18 (`plan/reader-read-18.md`): seven
chapters under three concepts, every one ending with its person worse off or merely offered
something, and the operator not hooked. The concept is drawn once and run in both persons,
first and third, the same writer and nothing else different, because read 17 §3.2 and read 18
§2.3 route the register's surviving families to the person flip and the flip is the operator's
position to reverse (read 4 §3, §195.2). Two experiments, `runs/ab/pilot24-first/` and
`runs/ab/pilot24-third/`; two listings, one per person, from the one concept.

## 0. What this is, and what it is not

One writer, `marsh`, the genre-centre dossier with no stance, so the person is decided by the
flag alone. Three exemplars, the strip live, reviser off. **n is one per arm.** Two draws under
one concept are two descriptions, never a treatment effect (`serial-pilot-15b.md` §0); the
coordinator's read is a diagnostic and never evidence (§95); the operator's read is what the
pair answers to, and his question of read 18 — what would hook him — is what it is for.

## 1. The concept, as drawn

Empty brief, no person asked (the person is the variable), the three exemplars' listings
shown, `--scenes 6`. One call, no readers, no title. Settled at
`runs/pilots/pilot24/concept.json` (gitignored) and copied byte for byte into both listing
folders; recorded here verbatim:

> Who they were the day before: the overnight desk clerk at a self-storage yard, twenty-four,
> who spent every shift timing himself picking practice locks he bought with his own money.
>
> What they alone have, from the first chapter: his class sheet has a fourth line where
> everyone else's has three, and the fourth line is blank — he is the only person on Earth the
> Standing asks instead of tells, and the only one who can write a class that was not offered,
> at a price it quotes him first.
>
> The first time it works, in chapter one: offered Sentinel, Forager, or Runner while the
> roll-up door of unit B-114 lifts from the inside, he writes LOCKSMITH on the blank line; the
> Standing quotes the price as one rung — he will begin below every other person alive — waits
> for him to say yes, and says Acknowledged.
>
> What they want, in their own words: I want to be the guy they call when nobody else can get
> it open.
>
> The system, the Standing. How it shows itself: speaks as a counterparty, not a god: it makes
> an offer, names the price, waits for a spoken yes, answers with the single word Acknowledged,
> and never explains or repeats itself; what it wants is for him to keep writing on the line.
>
> What it looks like: cold white text with no frame or border, hanging a hand's width in front
> of whatever he is looking at, and a hairline seam of blue light along every door-frame,
> hatch, lid and jamb within sight, so the world reads as a place made of openings.
>
> How far up it goes: 10 steps. Where the strongest person anyone has heard of stands: the
> first broadcast names one living holder of rung eight in the local cluster and states that
> as of hour one nobody on Earth stands above rung one.
>
> What a step up buys: safety — each rung raises your Threshold, the highest-rung thing the
> Standing will permit through a door you hold, so a rung is a wall you can stand behind.
>
> What kills people here, in the first days: Claimants: at integration every doorway on Earth
> became a contested boundary, and anything that steps through a threshold nobody holds takes
> the space and everything breathing inside it.
>
> Where it first reaches them: unit B-114 in the yard's back row, whose roll-up door begins
> opening from the inside at 3:40 a.m. while he is still reading his sheet.
>
> The turn, after the first arc: he learns the blank fourth line is not a gift but a seat:
> every integrated world produces exactly one write-in, the previous one is alive, holds rank
> in the Standing, and is coming to close the line.
>
> The first arc opens: 3:12 a.m. at the yard desk, practice cylinder in his hands, when his
> phone, the gate monitor, and the microwave clock all show the same six words at the same
> moment.
>
> Its middle: a customer stranded at the gate compares sheets with him under the sodium light
> — hers has three lines and a countdown, his has four and no countdown — and she is the first
> person to say out loud that his is wrong.
>
> It closes: he says yes to the price, drops to rung zero as LOCKSMITH takes, and the B-114
> door finishes opening on something that has to come through a threshold he now holds.
>
> What the book owes, and the scene each is due by:
> - the other four people on the yard at 3:12 a.m.: where each of them was standing when the
>   doors got seams, and which of them is still alive at dawn (by scene 3)
> - unit B-114: who rented it, what was in it before the world changed, and whether the
>   Claimant came from inside it or only used it (by scene 4)
> - the price of the write-in: what rung zero actually costs at a door — shown, not stated,
>   with him on the wrong side of a Threshold (by scene 5)
> - the fourth line: the Standing's own stated reason for asking him instead of telling him,
>   in its own words and no others (by scene 6)

**What the coordinator sees in it, before anything is drawn from it.** The four fields did
what they were asked and the concept is a different kind of book from the three before it: a
power a reader of this shelf would want (write your own class), used and working inside
chapter one at a price he says yes to out loud; a threat that takes rooms and the people in
them, reaching him in the first chapter through a door opening from the inside; a prize a
reader can feel (a rung is a wall you can stand behind); a system that is a counterparty with
a look (cold white text; a blue seam along every door in sight); a want in the words he had
before (*the guy they call when nobody else can get it open*); a turn that is a hunter. The
concept's own words carry *rung* seven times, which is this house's machinery word; the
listing loop's rail refuses it only as a name, and the seed will call the ladder what it
calls it. One thing to watch at the read: the price (*he will begin below every other person
alive*) is the accumulation shape §136 measured as box-ticking when a rule produced it; here
the writer chose it, and whether it reads as a real price is the read's to say.
