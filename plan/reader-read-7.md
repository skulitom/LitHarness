# The seventh operator read — protocol, fixed before the book was put in front of anybody

**Status: PROTOCOL ONLY, 2026-08-29. §4 is empty and is the read.**

In the form of the first six ([`reader-read-6.md`](reader-read-6.md) is the immediate
precedent): the operator's words quoted and not paraphrased, the analysis ours, and one standing
question asked of every defect.

**§§0–3 are written before the read**, so the package, the prompts and the routing rules are
fixed in advance and cannot be arranged around what comes back. That ordering is the whole point
of the form and is the only reason a defect harvest is worth writing down at all. §4 stays empty
until the operator has read the book.

**What is new here is the writer.** *The Rainwright's Apprentice Has No Licence* is the first
book by a **recruited** writer rather than the compiled cast — `larkin`, the Light Fantasy
recruit the operator accepted onto the roster on 2026-08-28. Read 5 §4.4 recorded a capability
question about the system — *can this setup produce light fantasy at all?* — and this is the
first artifact that bears on it. §3's fourth route is where that belongs, and §0 says what the
book can and cannot settle about it.

## 0. What this read is, and what it is not

**It is a defect harvest and it is not data** (§95's scope axiom). Nothing the operator says
becomes a prompt, a directive, a finding or a plan item directly (§97.1, and the `debug-book`
rule). Every defect goes through §3's question instead.

**No model chose what is in the package or the order it is read in** (§84). §1 lists what exists;
depth and order are the operator's. No model ranked, selected or judged anything at any point in
the run that produced this book — not the listing, not the world, not the prose
([`serial-pilot-13.md`](serial-pilot-13.md) records where each judgment call was refused, and
the two places where one was made by a person instead).

**No book here is being compared to another.** Every other book on the shelf was made under
different inputs — different writer, different world, different gate — so a counter beside a
counter is a description of two books and never a difference between two treatments. In
particular **`larkin` versus `ferreira` is not a comparison**: the writer, the world, the seed
and the number of seeds all differ at once.

**It is not a quality claim.** §61's bar is a blinded, position-swapped win rate against matched
published prose. This is one chapter of one book, unblinded by construction.

**On the capability question, what this book can and cannot settle.** It is one draw by one
writer under an empty brief. It can show that a light-fantasy listing and chapter are *reachable*
by this setup; it cannot say the setup reliably produces them, and it measures nothing. Read 5
§4.4's two separating tests — the tonal audit of our own text, and a brief-versus-empty arm —
are still the things that would tell *won't, by default* from *can't, when asked*, and neither
was run here.

**The reading copy is not blinded and carries a progress table and a revision id.** That does not
make what the operator notices wrong; it means a note from here is evidence of the same class as
the first six reads. Under §95 the blinded class is unreachable, so this is not a weaker version
of better evidence that could be bought later — it is a different thing entirely.

**One direction already arrived before this read, and it is not a §4 item.** The operator saw the
book mid-run and named a standing genre constraint — that we should not be writing books outside
LitRPG. That is recorded as a direction in [`serial-pilot-13.md`](serial-pilot-13.md) §8 and is
being settled in `plan/house-genre-constraint.md`, **not** routed through §3 here: a constraint
the operator states is direction entering its designed channel, where a defect quote is not. It
is noted in this section so §§0–3 stay honest about what was known before §4 was written, and so
nobody later mistakes the ordering.

**One thing about this book's world the operator should know before reading, because it is ours
and not the book's.** The world was accepted over a single tolerated complaint from a documented
tool defect, and the operator-facing `world ladders` view prints empty on it even though the
world's three ladders resolve in canon. Neither fact reaches the page.
[`serial-pilot-13.md`](serial-pilot-13.md) §3 has it in full.

## 2. The three prompts, fixed before the read

These are the behaviours the instruments try to simulate, and the operator is the one reader
whose behaviour may be watched at all (§97.1 diagnostics). None of them asks for a score.

1. **Covers and title.** Which would you click, and which would you not — with the sentence why.
2. **The listing.** Start reading or not, and the sentence why.
3. **Chapter 1.** **Where you stopped, and why.** Not a rating, not a verdict at the end — the
   place you stopped. If you did not stop, that is an answer and the sentence why still matters.

**On prompt 1, one thing read 5 established the hard way:** a ranking with no reason attached is
the shape this project has the least use for, because it can only be acted on by guessing what
the chosen covers have in common, and guessing what a reader meant is the judge channel with
extra steps. The sentence is the part that has to come.

**On prompt 3, what read 6 did not receive.** *Where you stopped* went unanswered last time, so
the one behaviour this form has never exercised is still unexercised. It is asked again here,
unchanged and not rephrased to make it easier — rephrasing it to get an answer would change what
was asked.

**A second reader is admitted if the operator vouches for one**, per stage-0 §148 and read 6
§4.0: a household read the operator counts as part of their own judgment is part of this harvest,
attributed, and inherits exactly the harvest's standing — defect harvest, not measurement. The
measurement channel stays closed.

## 3. The standing question, and the routes

Of every defect: **did any directive forbid it, or is this a gap?** The answer routes one of
three ways, written beside the quote:

- **enforcement defect** — a standing rule forbids it and the book did it anyway. Read 6 §4.6 is
  the worked example: the rule existed, the book did not do it, and the question became *why the
  clause did not land*, never a second clause on top;
- **direction gap** — nothing asked. Reads 1–3's usual answer. **A new clause is not the
  reflex**: §127, §135 and §138 are the three entries on what clause-adding costs, and read 4
  found the opposite failure — three standing instructions actively suppressing what the operator
  wanted. Read 5 §4.3 and read 6 §4.2 both found gaps whose cause was in our own instruction text
  rather than in the model, which is the §116 shape and the one to check for first;
- **instrument question** — the defect is real and nothing counts it. Routes to the battery
  families in [`reader-perception-research.md`](reader-perception-research.md): does an admitted
  damage family cover it, and if not, is there a mechanical key that could? It does **not** route
  to a new ad-hoc metric (`BRIEF.md` first).

**And a fourth outcome, admitted since read 5 §4.4 needed it and the form had no slot.** A
**capability question about the system** — *can this setup produce X at all?* — is neither a
defect nor a preference, and forcing it into one of the three routes answers a different question
than the one asked. Where an item is a doubt about the machine's range rather than a fault in the
book, it is recorded as that. **This read is the first with an artifact standing against such a
question**, so an item here may also *discharge* one — and if it does, §0's limits on what one
draw can settle apply to the discharge as much as to the doubt.

**And one boundary, so nobody trips on §97.1 later.** A brief supplied by the operator is the
**designed input channel**; direction entering there is not a diagnostic being laundered into a
prompt. What would be laundering is mining the defect quotes for prompt language. The line: the
operator's direction may enter where operator input was always designed to enter, and nowhere
else.
