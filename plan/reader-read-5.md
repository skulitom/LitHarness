current architecture; the books on the shelf were made under different inputs, one of them under

These are the behaviours the instruments try to simulate, and the operator is the one reader

3. **Chapters** (only if they are drafted): **where you stopped, and why.** Not a rating, not a

## 3. The standing question, and the three routes

Of every defect: **did any directive forbid it, or is this a gap?** The answer routes one of
three ways, written beside the quote:

- **enforcement defect** — a standing rule forbids it and the book did it anyway;
- **direction gap** — nothing asked. Reads 1–3's usual answer. **A new clause is not the
  reflex**: §127, §135 and §138 are the three entries on what clause-adding costs, and read 4
  found the opposite failure — three standing instructions actively suppressing what the operator
  wanted;
- **instrument question** — the defect is real and nothing counts it. Routes to the battery
  families in [`reader-perception-research.md`](reader-perception-research.md): does an admitted
  damage family cover it, and if not, is there a mechanical key that could? It does **not** route
  to a new ad-hoc metric (`BRIEF.md` first).

### 4.1 *"it's kind of like a list with constant 'and then', 'and then'"*

> *"'Every screen on Earth lit with the same message in the same second, and the message said
> that killing the things now coming through would make a person stronger. Then the doors
> opened, in car parks and stairwells and drained swimming pools, and the monsters climbed
> out.' It's not really engaging writing, it's kind of like a list with constant "and then",
> "and then""*

**Measured, because it is measurable**, against the 60-listing admitted market pool the
instruments already use:

| | pilot 11 | market pool of 60 |
| --- | --: | --- |
| `and`/`then` tokens per 100 words | **6.48** | median 2.99, p25–p75 1.79–3.66, **max 5.88** |
| worst single sentence, count of `and` | **3** | 34 listings max out at 1, 23 at 2, **only 2 reach 3** |
| share of sentences opening `Then`/`And`/`Now` | 0.20 | median 0.10, max 0.25 — pilot 11 above 54 of 60 |
| a **then-chain**: one sentence that both opens on a sequential coordinator and carries 2+ `and` | **1** | **1 of 60 listings contains one** |

**On raw coordinator density this listing is above every one of the 60.** Three of the four
measures put it at or past the market's ceiling; the fourth, sequential openers on their own,
only reaches the 90th percentile, which is worth recording because it is the one that does *not*
separate — sentence-initial `Then` is ordinary in this market and is not what he noticed.

**Read, not just counted** — pilot 7 §4.1's precedent, where a counter of and-stretched sentences
had to be read before it could be routed. The five sentences carry 1, **3**, 0, 0 and 2 `and`s.
The whole of the defect is the second sentence, and its cause is legible: it is doing the entire
world-setup — doors, three example locations, monsters — as one compound list. The listing's
other four sentences are unremarkable. So this is one sentence's construction, not a house style.

**Did any directive forbid it? No — this is a direction gap.** The listing role carries fifteen
explicit demands and not one reaches clause-joining: they govern where to open, exactness,
ambiguity, naming the genre's furniture, paragraph cohesion, dashes, person and length.
`house.CLARITY` reaches sentences and paragraphs and stops there — follow-on-first-read, unmet
terms needing a consequence, no two-way sentences, objects not acting, one pronoun per person.
**A chained listing passes every one of those clauses individually.** The only instruction in
this project's history that would have caught it — pilot 1's register directive, *"When a
sentence grows, cut it into two rather than joining clauses with commas and subordinators"* — was
a per-book `tone_note` on `serial.db`, and that channel is closed.

**This is the second read's lesson one level up, and `house.CLARITY` says so in its own last
line.** That clause ends *"...has been thrown out of the book, **and the sentences were all
fine**"* — sentence rules could not see a paragraph defect, which is why the paragraph clause
exists. Here the paragraph clause holds and the paragraph drones: it coheres, and it plods.

**The route is not a sixteenth clause.** §127, §135 and §138 are three entries on what
clause-adding costs, the listing prompt has already been measured cramming under its own demand
count, and what worked in §138 was subtraction and a gate. What this defect has that most do not
is **a distribution, a side, and no model in the loop**: the market pool is on disk, the
statistic is arithmetic. That makes it a candidate for the counter-and-gate route, and possibly
for the demand-count arithmetic instead — rhythm is a plausible thing to collapse when demands
cram. Nothing is built here and no bar is declared.


#### 4.1.1 Corrected the same evening, against sixteen draws already paid for

Two claims above were wrong and one hypothesis died. The correction is made here rather than by
editing the paragraphs, so what was claimed stays visible. The data is `derived/listing-arms*.json`
— pilot 10's sixteen arm draws, whose prose and whose per-arm **demand counts** were both already
on disk, so none of this cost a call.

**Correction 1.** ~~*"and is the highest of our own four"*~~ — true only of the four *books*.
Across all **21 listings this project has on disk**, pilot 11 is **third**: `no_genre/ferreira`
reaches **7.69** and `clarity/vance` **6.67**, both above pilot 11's 6.48.

**Correction 2.** ~~*"So this is one sentence's construction, not a house style."*~~ — the
opposite. **3 of our 21 listings exceed the market's maximum** and **7 of 21 exceed its p90**
(4.32). The operator pointed at pilot 11 because pilot 11 is what he was shown; the property is
standing, and about a third of every listing this project draws sits above the market's ninetieth
percentile on it.

**And the hypothesis that would have licensed the obvious fix is refuted.** The a-priori
favourite was §138's cramming mechanism — sixteen demands for a hundred-word artifact, met by
compressing four clauses into one 79-word sentence — which would have made **subtraction** the
fix, as it was there. Pilot 10's arms vary demand count (9 in `base` and `no_genre`, 11 in
`clarity` and `genre_clarity`), so the prediction is testable on paid data:

| arm | demands | mean C per 100 words |
| --- | --: | --: |
| `base` | 9 | 3.78 |
| `no_genre` | 9 | 4.33 |
| `clarity` | 11 | 3.91 |
| **`genre_clarity` — the shipped arm** | 11 | **3.06** |

**Correlation between demand count and coordinator density over the sixteen draws: r = −0.18.**
Slightly *negative*, on a narrow 9-versus-11 range, and **the shipped arm is the best of the four
on this counter.** Cramming does not explain the parataxis, and **subtraction is not licensed by
this evidence.** A negative result on the favourite is the useful part of running it.

**So the counter-and-gate route is the one left standing, and its bar is attainable.** The four
checks §81, §85, §87 and §89 each bought, run at the real n before any bar is declared, with the
threshold at **the market's own maximum, 5.88 per 100 words**:

| check | result |
| --- | --- |
| range at the real n | market 0.00–5.88 over 60; ours 1.79–7.69 over 21 — both sides populated |
| direction | pre-specified: lower is less list-like |
| independent unit | one listing per draw; no listing appears twice |
| non-empty subgroup | **3 of our 21 would be refused; 0 of the market's 60 would be** |

A threshold at the market **maximum** rather than at p90 is the deliberate choice: at p90 the
gate would refuse a tenth of the listings this market actually rewards, and §116's lesson is that
a recall-tuned list run as a refusal gate has inverted error costs. At the maximum it refuses only
what nothing in the market does. 18 of our 21 draws already pass it, so a refused draw has a high
prior of landing under the bar on the redraw.

**What still stands between that and a shipped fix, named rather than assumed.** The listing
loop's existing gate is `passed=True, blocking=False` — it *reports* and has never refused
anything, so a refusing gate is a new behaviour with a redraw cost, not a threshold swap. And
**RS1 is the real constraint**: the bar above is derived from the market pool, and nothing under
`src/litharness/` may reference a corpus. Whether a frozen scalar counts as a reference is a
question for whoever builds it, and it is a question rather than an obstacle — §104's platform
priors are the precedent to check first. Nothing is built here.

### 4.2 *"isn't that basically every litrpg"* — the hook and the voice

> *"'message said that killing the things now coming through would make a person stronger' -
> isn't that basically every litrpg, we want to show the author voice and grip the readers with
> a hook, this doesn't accomplish either of these"*

**Route: instrument question, and this is its second sighting.** The first was not the operator:
`handoff-listing-loop.md` task 2 records a measurement reader on the 1/4 listing saying *"every
beat here is one I've read a dozen times — corporate guy, menu in the eyes, climb the tower"*,
filed then as **genre-cliché fatigue, and nothing counts it**. Named once by a model reader and
once by the operator, months apart, with nothing built in between.

**It is its own family and must not be folded into the causal-salience battery.** That battery
locates *within-text* damage against a hidden key inside one story. *"Every LitRPG opens this
way"* is **between-text typicality** — a property of this premise against a population — and no
key hidden inside the text can certify it. Its architectural kin already exists in this
repository: the **comparative-reading-with-a-reference-shelf** family, `blurb_shelf`'s shape,
where the population sits in context and membership is checkable by code. Pointed at premise and
hook rather than at register, that is the mechanism class this defect belongs to.

**The voice half is worse off than the hook half**, because its instrument is already dead:
§137's `writer_distinctness` returned `DISTINCT_BUT_ORDER_BLIND` with both halves of its
calibration empty. But the death was **preference-shaped** — rank these writers — and the
survivor in that territory is §87–§89's E6 form: *name the difference* survives where *prefer a
side* is VOID. So "show the author voice" is not without a shape; it is without a preference
instrument, which is a different and better problem. The tells-battery's derived-lexicon idea is
the third relative.

Neither half routes to a metric proposal (`BRIEF.md` first) or to a clause, and both take the
same validation discipline as everything else: the follower gradient before any reading of ours
is believed.

### 4.3 *"extremely close to the ferreira"* — and the cause is in our own text

> *"This story premises is extremely close to the ferreira, makes me worried about the creative
> capacity of the current setup."*

**An honesty note first: this reaction was primed and is not independent corroboration.** The
seven-of-seven finding was put in front of the operator in the same message that carried the
listing. It tells us the repetition is visible and that it matters to him; it is not a second,
separate detection. Nothing is lost by saying so — the 7-of-7 and 5-of-5 counts are facts about
artifacts on disk and carry the convergence claim on their own.

**The worry names the model. The measurement names us.** This is §116's shape — the operator
refused three forged worlds as *"depressing and incredibly boring"* and the bias turned out to be
in our `_RULES`, not in the model. Here it is in the dossiers. Each of the four carries more than
the writer's *appetite*: each **names an inciting beat**, and each writer then draws that beat
every time.

| writer | the beat their own dossier names | what every listing opens on | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ferreira` | *"an ordinary world is pulled into something vastly larger and everybody finds out at the same moment"*, *"the first message nobody asked for"* | a message reaching every screen on Earth in the same second | 7 of 7 |
| `halloran` | *"people who wake up somewhere impossible and have to survive the afternoon"*, *"the first monster, the first spell that goes wrong"* | a thing in a dark stairwell, and the light going | 5 of 5 |
| `vance` | *"somebody who died badly and woke up as something else"*, *"a beast that should have killed the main character and is theirs instead"* | a thing out of the dark that should have killed the narrator and did not | 4 of 4 |
| `okonjo` | *"sects, masters worth impressing, rivals"*, *"the technique somebody is not supposed to have"* | a sect entrance or duel decided by a forbidden breathing form | 4 of 4 |

**What the counts count**, because two defensible numbers exist and only one is in the table.
The right-hand column counts **distinct listings** — a writer's final text per draw. Counting
the two `ferreira` drafts that differ from their own revisions it is **9 of 9**, and that is the
more interesting number: **the beat survives the revision step as well as the draw.** Say which
one is meant wherever it is cited; stage-0 §146.1 carries the seven, with the correction from
eight.

**`writers.py`'s own docstring already warned about the previous version of this defect.** The
first cast named four real careers and *"each writer promptly set a book inside their own day
job"* — G3 contamination, four worlds with no magic in them. The fix made the dossier's variable
*appetite* rather than profession. **The leak reproduced one level up:** appetite as written also
fixes the inciting incident, so the profession stopped leaking and the opening beat started.

So on this evidence the answer to *"creative capacity of the current setup"* is that the setup is
not failing to invent — **it is drawing what it was told to draw, four writers over.** That is a
text problem rather than a model problem, and a better one to have.

Route: **direction gap with its cause located in our own instruction text**, the §116 shape. Not
an enforcement defect — nothing forbids a writer reusing a premise — and not an instrument
question, because the cause was found by reading our own text rather than by needing a new
measurement. What to do about it is a roster act and belongs to the operator
(`plan/writer-roster.md`). Nothing is changed here.

### 4.4 *"concerned we build a system that is not capable of producing this"*

> *"I like light fantasy, things that are less grimdark, i feel like currently it's
> underrepresented."*

and, correcting the first reading of it the same evening:

> *"I didn't say Light Fantasy missing was a defect, I was just concerned we build a system that
> is not capable of producing this."*

**This is neither a defect nor a preference, and it does not fit any of §3's three routes.** It
is a **capability question about the system**: *can this setup produce light fantasy at all?*
Recorded as such rather than forced into a route, because forcing it would answer a different
question than the one asked. That §3's three routes had no slot for it is itself a finding about
the protocol — the form was built assuming every item a read returns is a defect, and this one
is a doubt about the machine's range.

**The honest evidential state is that we cannot yet tell two very different answers apart.**
Every listing this project has ever drawn ran under an **empty brief**, and all four dossiers are
disaster-shaped. So *"won't, by default"* and *"can't, when asked"* both predict exactly the
artifacts on disk, and nothing on disk separates them. Writing that down is the substance of this
entry; anything more confident would be invented.

**Two tests separate them, and one is already half-run.**

1. **Is the default manufactured by our own text?** The tonal audit, dossiers first
   (`domain/writers.py`). **The §116-style *lexical* audit comes back null and is recorded as a
   result**: a threat/grim word family over the four dossiers, the listing role's rule text and
   the house floor returns 1–3 hits per dossier, 1 in `house.CLARITY`, 1 in `house.HOUSE_RULES`,
   0 in `house.READER` and `house.ACCUMULATION`; the one apparent "light" hit is *"the kind of
   serial"* and is a false positive. There is no grimdark *wording* the way the retired forge's
   `_RULES` carried administrative wording. (No reusable instrument existed to borrow — §116's
   own audit lived substantially in the forge, retired at `5483161`.) **The *structural* half of
   the same audit is positive**: the four dossiers name *system apocalypse*, *portal fantasy and
   dungeons*, *reincarnation as something else*, and *cultivation, sects and tournaments*. Every
   one is threat-forward by construction, and **not one names comedy, warmth, low stakes or
   anything a reader would call light.** So *"underrepresented"* is exact and, if anything,
   understated.
2. **Can it when asked?** A brief-versus-empty listing arm — same roster, one cheap registered
   arm, listing draws only, no drafting. It reads both whether a brief moves tone and whether a
   brief breaks the premise convergence of §4.3. **If a light brief still returns disaster
   listings, that is a genuine capability finding** and the operator's concern is confirmed
   rather than dismissed. Registration before any paid call, RUNBOOK first. Not run; operator's
   to authorise.

**What the evidence so far does point at is our text rather than the model's range**, on two
checkable grounds:

- **This very listing already contains a light-shaped idea, wearing dark dressing.** Its actual
  turn is *"He finds ways to get paid that involve no killing at all"* — a pacifist exploit run,
  which is about as un-grimdark as a premise gets — arriving after monsters climb out of drained
  swimming pools. The idea was available to the writer. Nothing chose against the dressing.
- **Every cover this project has ever generated is dark, and that is measurable.** Mean
  luminance over the eight finished covers on the shelf, on a 0–255 scale: *Copy Costs A Hand*
  35.2 and 26.5, *Patch Notes For Earth* 47.0 and 36.9, *The Unkillable Exploit* 50.8, 50.0,
  38.6, 53.4. **The brightest cover ever produced is 53.4 — barely a fifth of full scale — and
  not one of the eight reaches mid-grey.**

  **The link to the listing is architectural and is not measured, and the difference matters.**
  ~~The cover pipeline takes the listing as its description, so this is the skew *propagating*
  through a downstream layer rather than originating in one.~~ What is true: the pipeline
  **does** read the listing as its description (`application/covers.py` takes `--bundle`'s
  listing), so a path from listing to cover exists by construction. What is **not** shown is any
  dose-response along it — all eight covers came from dark listings, so the sample has **no
  variation on the input** and cannot say whether a lighter listing yields a lighter cover. The
  eight are a consistent skew across three layers, which is what an upstream text cause would
  look like; they are not evidence that fixing the upstream fixes the cover.

  **So nothing done to the dossiers may be read as having fixed the covers**, and testing that
  link is its own leg with its own cost: cover luminance needs covers *generated*, which is
  image-model spend on top of any listing sweep. It does not ride along free.

**One trap, named before anyone reaches for it.** The obvious move — pass `light fantasy` as the
brief — is the move `--brief`'s own help text warns against in as many words: *"NOT a shelf label
— §136 measured the two words `progression fantasy` outweighing every rule in the prompt."* A
brief is a story, a situation, or a constraint somebody cares about. Two words of shelf label is
a failure this project has already bought once, and the arm in test 2 has to be designed around
it or it will measure the label rather than the question.

**And one boundary, so nobody trips on §97.1 later.** A brief supplied by the operator is the
**designed input channel** — briefs were always operator-supplied, pilot 3 ran under one, and
§136 measured that channel's power. Direction entering there is not a diagnostic being laundered
into a prompt. What would be laundering is mining the defect quotes above for prompt language.
The line: **the operator's direction may enter where operator input was always designed to enter,
and nowhere else.**

**No stage-0 number is claimed for this read.** Entries record what was measured and what
shipped, not what was wanted; a § gets claimed when something ships because of this — the
structural dossier finding acted on, or the brief arm run.

## 5. What the reader was given before reading, and why it is now a caveat

Recorded here because it is a property of the machinery rather than of this book, and because
without it a true reaction would be harvested as the wrong defect.

**Every listing this roster draws under an empty brief opens on that writer's single premise.**
`ferreira` has drawn seven and all seven open on a message arriving on every screen or phone on
Earth in the same second; `halloran` five, all on a thing in a dark stairwell that takes the
light; `vance` four; `okonjo` four. The full table, the sources and the epistemic status are in
[`serial-pilot-9-10.md`](serial-pilot-9-10.md) §4. Pilot 10's 2x2 moved the counters it varied
and moved no writer off its premise in any cell.

So *"I have read this opening before"* would be a true reaction to a real property — of the
writer under an empty brief, not of this listing.

**This is why §4.3's quote is marked primed.** Handing the finding over before the read was the
honest thing to do and it cost the read its independence on that one point; both halves of that
are recorded rather than one. §4.3 relocates the cause from the writer to the dossier, which is
where this section pointed and did not yet look.
