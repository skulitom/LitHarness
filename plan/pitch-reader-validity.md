# Pitch-reader validity — can a simulated reader tell a damaged premise from its original?

**Status: RUN, 2026-08-23. §6 carries the result: K1 and K2 pass, K4 fails, K3 is reported.**
Pre-registered before the first call, so the kill conditions could not be fitted to a result;
K1a is the one clause added mid-run and it names the number it had seen. Written before the first call, so
the kill conditions below cannot be fitted to a result. The instrument is
`research/quality-measurement/pitch_battery.py`; the damage is `ablate.PITCH_SET`; the ladder and
the elicitation are `persona_battery`'s, inherited rather than re-invented.

## 0. Why this exists, and the one thing it may not become

On 2026-08-23 the operator asked for a book, was shown six forged worlds over five forges, and
refused all six **on their premises alone** — never reaching prose. Three rule changes came out of
those refusals (stage-0 §116, §118, §119). Then the question that motivates this file:

> *"This is the reason we wanted to simulate readers, is this not what this whole project is about
> and why we are doing it? 'whether a reader would want the result' — this is the only important
> metric, it 100% should be what this project should do."*

That is the project's stated purpose (README: a simulated readership as the reward model), and
the honest position is that **no instrument in this repository can answer it yet**. Four candidate
measurements have been attempted and none produced a valid reading of taste; a panel asked for an
absolute verdict returned `keep-reading` on 195 of 196 draws; a verdict channel asked to prefer a
side ran 4,676× position over text (§87–§89). This file is a step toward the question, at the
cheapest place in the pipeline to ask it.

**It may not become a picker.** §61(5), §105.1 and §107.5 stand: no model ranks or selects among
candidates without the containment the log requires, and `forge --pick` stays a person's act
(`plan/world-architect.md` §2). A pass here licenses one thing — a **diagnostic printed beside the
gate complaints**, which the operator reads and may ignore. Nothing selects.

## 1. Why a pitch rather than prose

A drafted scene costs ~$0.30 and a book ~$6 plus half an hour; a forged premise is ~120 words and
exists the moment the forge returns. It is the earliest point in the pipeline where a reader can
be put, and — measured today — it is where the operator's own refusals actually happened.

It also sits on the only un-memorised source this project has (BRIEF.md §4): every premise here
was generated by this system today, so no scoring model has been trained on it, and Pass 6's
transferable rule (a model's familiarity swings a score harder than real damage) has nothing to
grip.

## 2. The design

**Units.** Six premises, forged 2026-08-23, from `pilot6/direct3`, `pilot6/direct5` and
`pilot6/direct6`. Two were forged before §118 and §119 landed and four after, which is a
difference this design does **not** use as a variable — the arms are within-pitch.

**Arms** (`ablate.PITCH_SET`), each applied at full dose to each premise:

| arm | sign | what it does | reaches |
|---|--:|---|--:|
| `admin_frame` | −1 | §116's vocabulary put back, by fixed substitution | 3 of 6 |
| `jargonise` | −1 | the world's **own** coined terms in place of plain nouns | 6 of 6 |
| `ladder_first` | −1 | opens on the declared chain instead of on the person | 6 of 6 |
| `neutral_first` | 0 | `ladder_first`'s matched control: same deletion, a sentence about places | 6 of 6 |
| `rename_pitch` | sham | the world's own cast renamed | 6 of 6 |
| `respell_pitch` | sham | contraction and spelling variants | 5 of 6 |

**The fourth defect is absent and no result here speaks to it.** *"'wet cinder', 'because his body
rings' … are not things anybody says in any context"* — a premise written as mood rather than as a
pitch — cannot be manufactured without a rewriter, and `ablate`'s ground truth is code by standing
rule. §119 changed the rule that causes it; nothing here measures it.

**Response variable.** Blinded pairwise forced choice, both orientations, four personas,
`Elicitor.compare_pair`.

**The panel is `personas.GENRE_PANEL`, and it was written for this run.** The operator directed
it mid-build: the readers are to be enthusiasts of the eleven genres this project writes in —
progression fantasy, isekai, portal fantasy, cultivation, sci-fi, cozy, slow burn, LitRPG, fantasy
academia, superhero, modern supernatural — and *"extra critical about their tastes"*, refined to
***voracious*** readers rather than enthusiastic ones. Four
readers cover the eleven, separable by what each drops on. Criticality is written as demonstrated
taste rather than as an instruction, because gate 0's positivity floor happened against a system
prompt that already told the reader it was allowed to stop: **every persona here puts down at
least one book the genre loves**, and each states a real abandonment rate. `personas.PANEL` is
untouched so §70's numbers stay reproducible. The first run of this battery was killed after ~30
calls when the direction arrived, and nothing from it is reported.

**The scalar.** P(panel prefers the **damaged** premise). Damage is declared to *lower* it, and
0.5 is indifference.

**Why pairwise and not a rating.** `persona_battery` gate 0 measured the absolute verdict constant
— 195 of 196 `keep-reading`, `ms_between` and `ms_within` both exactly 0.0 — and §61's raw model
judges died to positional artifacts before it. A forced choice produces variance by construction
and is the only standing §70 concluded a persona panel could earn.

## 3. Kill conditions, pre-registered

**K1 — constancy.** If the six arms' mean preference rates span less than 0.05, the panel is a
constant function and every statistic below is undefined. This is gate 0's failure in its pitch
form and it kills the instrument outright.

**K1a — what K1 does *not* claim, and the operator's objection that produced this clause.**
*"Regards to rejecting everything, maybe the quality presented was just bad and that is why they
rejected."* Correct, and it is a real hole in how gate 0's floor was read: uniform rejection has
two explanations — a reader who refuses everything, and a set of texts that all deserved refusal —
and an **absolute** design cannot separate them. §70's 195-of-196 `keep-reading` has the same
ambiguity in the other direction and was recorded as a dead instrument when strictly it was
undetermined. The operator's own six refusals are the live example: they were not a reflex, they
produced three rule changes, and the forge run after them came back at 0.28 administrative words
per 1,000 against a prior median of 7.21.

**K1 survives the objection because nothing here is judged absolutely.** Every comparison is a
premise against *its own damaged copy*, so "the material was bad" moves both sides of the pair by
the same amount and cannot produce constancy. A panel that cannot separate a text from a version
of itself that was mechanically broken has failed at a task whose answer is known by construction.
K1 therefore kills the *instrument* and never the sample — and no result here may be read as
saying anything about whether the six premises are any good, which §4 already refuses on other
grounds.

*Written after the first cell of the run was visible on screen (`direct6:1|admin_frame`, 0.250).
It changes no threshold, no arm and no arithmetic; it records what K1 was always scoped to, in
answer to an objection that arrived mid-run. A pre-registration that is edited after seeing a
number has to say which number it had seen, so: that one.*

**K2 — the sham floor, read per sham and never pooled.** For each degrader,
`margin = |rate − 0.5| − max(|sham rate − 0.5|)`. At least one degrader must clear **+0.05**. A
degrader that does not beat the shams is measuring edited-ness. Pooling shams, or subtracting
`detect − sham` rather than taking absolute distances, is the arithmetic error BRIEF.md §2 Pass 6
records; `pitch_battery` computes the absolute form.

**K3 — position.** `elicit.positional_bias` is reported. If the positional effect exceeds the
largest degrader effect, the channel is position rather than text and the result is VOID — the
shape §89 recorded at 4,676×.

**K4 — the matched control.** `ladder_first` must sit further from indifference than
`neutral_first`. If they move the panel equally, the deletion did the work and the ladder reading
is unsupported. This is `destake` against `deplete_matched`, and it is why `neutral_first` exists.

**K5 — coverage is absence, not a null.** An arm reaching fewer than half the pitches reports what
it could not reach; `admin_frame` at 3 of 6 is already in that position, and the reason is itself a
§116 result: the amended rules removed the vocabulary this arm needs to find.

## 4. What a pass licenses, and what it does not

**Licensed:** printing a per-pitch diagnostic next to the forge's gate complaints, for the person
choosing. Nothing more.

**Not licensed, and named so it cannot be quietly assumed later:** that the panel can rank two
*undamaged* premises; that a premise it prefers is a better book; that any bar may be declared over
the rate; that the panel may choose among K candidates. Detecting manufactured damage is a
different claim from reading taste, and §87–§89 are three entries about the distance between them.

**A pass would also not make the operator's six refusals data.** §95 stands: they are direction.
Validating this instrument against them would convert them into labels, which is the one thing the
scope axiom closes, and it is not done here.

## 5. The run

```bash
uv run python research/quality-measurement/pitch_battery.py \
  --forge pilot6/direct6/forge.json pilot6/direct5/forge.json pilot6/direct3/forge.json \
```

paid arm, pilot loop or forge while it runs.

### 6.0 `jargonise` is withdrawn, and with it the only arm that cleared K2

Written, passed and withdrawn the same day. §6.4 named its confound and kept it; the operator read
that and refused the compromise — *"I don't think jargonise is a smart engineering move, it does
add to confusion. We shouldn't have things in our project that make things worse."* The arm
substitutes coined terms for plain nouns and so damages **sense** as well as vocabulary, and no
sham in the set controls for coherence, so its 0.0833 cannot be read as a reader noticing jargon.
Removed from `ablate.PITCH_SET`, kept as code with the reason in its docstring.

**The numbers below are left exactly as they were measured**, `jargonise` included, because a
withdrawn arm's figures are how a later reader checks the withdrawal. What changes is what may be
concluded, and §6.6 states it.

### 6.1 The kill conditions, in order

**K1 — constancy: PASSED.** The arms span 0.0833 to 0.5312, a range of **0.448** against a floor
of 0.05. The panel is not a constant function, which is the failure that killed gate 0's absolute
form. The shape is the one a working instrument has: shams near indifference, degraders far below
it.

**K2 — the sham floor: PASSED, decisively on one arm.** Worst sham effect **0.0625**.
`jargonise` clears it by **+0.3542** against a required +0.05. For scale, the prose pairwise cell
§70 records cleared at margin 0.1223 with a sham effect of 0.2833; this run's shams move a
quarter as much and its best degrader moves further.

**K3 — position: not void, and not nothing.** `chose_A_rate` **0.6725** over 229 decided
comparisons, and every persona shows it: climber 0.6964, regular 0.6897, mechanism 0.6667,
stranger 0.6379. The pre-registered condition voids the result if the positional effect exceeds
the largest degrader effect; 0.1725 against 0.4167 does not. **The win rates are already
orientation-corrected** — `Comparison.chose_variant` undoes the swap — so this is reported as a
property of the channel rather than as a correction owed. A panel that reaches for position two
times in three is a panel whose thin effects mean nothing, and only `jargonise` here is thick.

**K4 — the matched control: FAILED, and this is the finding the control was built to produce.**
`ladder_first` sits 0.3542 from indifference and its matched control `neutral_first` sits
**0.3958** — *further*. Both arms delete the premise's opening sentence; only one replaces it with
the world's declared chain. The panel reacted to **losing the sentence that introduces the
person**, and the chain was, if anything, marginally less unwelcome than a sentence about places.

**So the ladder-as-concept defect (§119) is untested and no reading of this run may claim it.**
What the run does say is a different thing worth having: the opening sentence of a premise is
load-bearing enough that removing it is detected at 0.396 by four independent readers — which is
§112's protagonist-first finding arriving from the reader's side rather than the forge's.

**K5 — coverage: `admin_frame` reports absence, not a null.** It reached **3 of 6** premises,
because §116's rule changes removed from the forge exactly the vocabulary this arm needs to find.
Its +0.0833 margin stands on three units and is not read as a result.

### 6.4 What this does not show, named rather than left for a later reader

**`jargonise` is confounded with sense.** It substitutes coined terms for plain nouns, which
damages meaning as well as adding vocabulary — *"he wants a groundfast that holds"*. The shams
control for edited-ness and for renaming, and **neither controls for coherence**. So the honest
claim is that a panel rejects a premise studded with the world's own coinages; whether it is
reacting to jargon fatigue or to damaged sense is not separated here, and an arm that substitutes
equally unfamiliar words that are *not* the world's would separate them.

**One dose, no monotonicity.** Everything ran at full strength. Detection at 1.0 alone is
detection of vandalism (`ablate`'s own docstring), and the dose ladder is the first thing to add.

**Six premises, all forged the same day by one model on one brief family.** No claim about
premises in general.

**Nothing here ranks two undamaged premises**, which is the thing that would actually answer *"is
this idea any good"*. Detecting manufactured damage and reading taste are different claims and
§87–§89 are three entries about the distance between them.

### 6.6 What stands after the withdrawal, and it is not a passing instrument

With `jargonise` out, the surviving degraders are:

- **`admin_frame`** — margin +0.0833, on **3 of 6** premises. K5 already said this reports absence
  rather than a result, and it is not promoted now that it is the only degrader left standing.
- **`ladder_first`** — margin +0.2917 and **K4 failed**: its matched control moved the panel
  further than it did.

**So no arm clears the ladder cleanly, and the honest verdict on this battery is a null.** What
survives is narrower and worth keeping:

1. **The panel is not constant** (K1, span 0.448) and **its shams behave** (0.0625 worst effect,
   with the personas *disagreeing* on them while agreeing on damage). The instrument has range —
   which is more than gate 0's absolute form ever showed.
2. **Removing a premise's opening sentence is detected at 0.396 by four independent readers**,
   whatever replaces it. That is `neutral_first`, which was built to be a control and returned the
   run's most robust effect.
3. **The reason codes work as direction** (§6.3), and cost nothing.

A null on the arms is the result. It is recorded as one rather than rescued by promoting the
coverage-limited arm, which is the move BRIEF.md §2 exists to make embarrassing.

### 6.5 An arithmetic bug in this battery's own summary, found by reading its output

The first summary reported `worst_sham_effect: 0.0` beside two shams that had plainly moved
(0.5312 and 0.4375). The sham lookup named `rename_entities` and `respell` — the standing pair —
after the arms had been renamed `rename_pitch` and `respell_pitch` for reaching a 120-word premise
at all, so the set was empty and **every margin was reported against zero**. That is BRIEF.md §2
Pass 6's inflated-margin error arriving by a different road, in the battery written to respect it.
Fixed by reading the sham keys off `ablate.PITCH_SHAMS`, and the corrected numbers are the ones in
§6. The replay cost nothing: the run is digest-cached and was re-scored, not re-bought.


## 7. Comprehension — the operator's instrument, and its first run

*"Maybe we need to somehow test reader comprehension too? Like if they can accurately explain what
they read in different words then it has meaning."* Proposed 2026-08-23 after six forged premises
drew six complaints, and the complaints were one complaint: nobody could say what the words meant.
`research/quality-measurement/comprehension_battery.py`.

**Why it is admissible where preference is not.** §87–§89 recorded E1/E2 — prefer a side — VOID at
4,676x position over text, and **E6, name the difference, surviving**. Restating a premise in your
own words is E6's shape, and it has a property preference never had: the answers can be checked
**against each other**, so the scorer is set arithmetic and no model judges anything.

### 7.1 Design

Nine items — the eight premises forged on 2026-08-23 plus one human-written reference pitch — each
read independently by the four `GENRE_PANEL` readers, one call apiece. Four questions: what can
this person do that nobody else can, what is in the way, what kind of book is this, and quote
anything you could not follow. 36 calls, 17 minutes, **$0.96** — a tenth of what the preference
battery cost for a result it did not get.

**The reference item is a stimulus and not a label.** It is the operator's own worked example,
included so the numbers have something human-written to sit beside. Nothing scores a forged premise
against it, no bar is taken from it, and §95 is untouched: the operator's *judgments* remain
direction, and this is a text they wrote rather than a verdict they gave.

### 7.2 Result: the confusion half works, the agreement half is a null

| premise | readers confused | unclear items |
|---|--:|--:|
| A Voice That Carries | **0 / 4** | **0** |
| Everything Here Is a Bell | 1 / 4 | 1 |
| *reference: the operator's example* | 2 / 4 | 5 |
| Wild Stock | 2 / 4 | 5 |
| Deep Enough to Carry | 3 / 4 | 12 |
| The Cold Palm | 4 / 4 | 5 |
| Nothing Comes Out Lighter | 4 / 4 | 8 |
| The Girl Who Worked in Daylight | 4 / 4 | 7 |
| The Slow Fire | 4 / 4 | 11 |

**The agreement scorer is dead.** Mean pairwise Jaccard over content words ran 0.048 to 0.171
across all nine items with the human-written reference **mid-pack at 0.132**. Readers instructed
not to quote the text back do not reuse each other's nouns either, so the overlap floor swamps the
signal. It separates nothing and is reported as a null rather than kept as a number.

**The confusion half is the instrument.** Four readers, working independently and blind to each
other, quoted back the same phrases the operator had objected to hours earlier — *"hard and
breakable, or soft and unbreakable"* (three of four), *"every crack he buys — in what sense does he
buy damage?"*, *"carrying four live grafts on a root that should feed two"*, and *"the fourth
thing"* (three of four). **This convergence is an observation and explicitly not a validation**:
scoring the instrument against the operator's judgments would convert them into labels, which §95
closes. It is recorded because a later reader would otherwise wonder whether anybody checked.

### 7.3 The confound the reference item exposed, and the fix it produced

The reference pitch drew 2 of 4 readers confused — and every item was a **question a pitch is
supposed to leave open**: *"what does the AI actually do for him"*, *"does he keep his adult
memories"*, *"is that a hard rule or flavour text"*. The forged premises drew a different species:
*"I don't know what a whip is"*, *"what does take-back-to-green mean"*, *"what the Cooling Yard
is"*.

**One counter could not separate a withheld hook from an unexplained word**, which is why the
human-written item landed mid-table. `did_not_follow` is therefore split into `undefined_words` and
`open_questions`, with the second reported beside the first and never added to it: a premise that
leaves a reader with questions it plans to answer is working, not broken. The split is the fix the
reference item bought, and it is the reason a comparison item was in the schedule at all.

### 7.4 What it does not measure

**Agreement is not quality even when it works**, and this run's agreement did not work. Four
readers can restate a dull premise perfectly. Comprehension is the floor under taste, not taste:
a premise its own target readers cannot restate has failed before the question of whether anybody
wants it is reached.

**No bar is declared** over the confusion count, and none may be read in. The distribution is nine
items on one day from one forge family, and the split above changes what the counter counts, so the
numbers in §7.2 are superseded as a scale by the next run rather than standing as one.


### 7.5 Run 2: the split, and a within-text control that passes

12 items, 48 calls, 25 minutes, **$2.31**. `results/comprehension2.json`. Ten forged premises
spanning three rule generations, plus a human-written reference pitch **and the same pitch with one
substitution** — `a Neuralink-like device` becomes `a kell-lattice`, a coined term nothing
explains. Everything else in the pair is byte-identical.

**The control passes, and it is the first control in this programme's reader work that has.**

| item | undefined words | readers flagging | open questions |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| reference pitch, as written | **1** | 1 / 4 | 11 |
| the same pitch, one term coined | **6** | 3 / 4 | 12 |

Three readers quoted **`kell-lattice`** back, unprompted and independently. The clean version drew
one item — the word *merged*. So the counter moves 6x on a single substitution in a 172-word text,
in the direction declared before the run.

**It also answers the risk the operator's own direction created.** `GENRE_PANEL` was written to be
*"extra critical about their tastes"*, and a critical reader flagging a clean text would have made
the counter worthless. One flag across four critical readers on an undamaged pitch is the measured
answer to that.

**And the split's pre-registered prediction held.** §7.3 said the reference item's five confusions
should reclassify as `open_questions` while the forged premises' should stay `undefined_words`.
They did: the reference now carries 11 open questions and 1 undefined word.

### 7.7 Three things this run says that no reading of it may soften

**Run 1 and run 2 are not comparable.** *A Voice That Carries* scored 0 unclear under *"anything
you could not follow"* and 16 under *"quote words used as if you already knew them"*. The second
wording invites listing. Only within-version comparisons hold, which is the reason the control pair
sits inside this batch rather than being inferred across runs.

~~**Clarity is not quality, and this run separates them by example.** The operator rated *A Voice
That Carries* the best of six premises at 9/100; it is the least comprehensible text in the table.~~

**Corrected the same day, by the operator, and the correction changes what this instrument is
for.** *"Clarity and quality are part of the same axis. A text cannot be considered high quality if
it's not clear."* That is right and the struck sentence was wrong twice over.

**The framing was wrong.** If clarity is necessary for quality then the two are not independent
axes — an unclear text has a **ceiling** on how good it can be, and comprehension measures one
necessary constituent of quality rather than something standing beside it.

**The evidence was worse than the framing.** *A Voice That Carries* was rated **9 out of 100**. A
9/100 is not a text of high quality whose clarity happens to be low; it is a bad text, and sixteen
undefined terms are part of why it is bad rather than a counterexample to the relation. Reading a
*within-batch preference* as *high quality* is the error, and the batch it won was one the operator
had just described as bottom-decile.

**What still stands, narrowed.** Comprehension is necessary and not sufficient: four readers can
parse a premise nobody would read, so a low undefined count does not make a premise good. The
direction is licensed — fewer unexplained terms is better, other things equal — and **no threshold
