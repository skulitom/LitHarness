\begin{abstract}
The sentence ``autonomous long-form generation cannot presently prove that its prose is
good'' is correct only if \emph{good} denotes universal aesthetic value. It is too strong if
\emph{good} denotes a declared, reader-grounded product claim. This paper defines a
Reader-Grounded Certificate (\RGC) for a content-addressed manuscript. The contract names
the target reader population, comparator distribution, exposure grain, outcome mapping,
quality thresholds, assay-validity controls, and a system-wide error budget before outcomes
exist. Blinded randomized panels then measure direct preference, voluntary continuation,
finish-and-recommend behavior, and the dramatic properties that matter to LitRPG: scene
function, costly progression, escalation and payoff, voice, and line-level craft. Outcomes
are aggregated at the reader level, not the scene level.

For each binary endpoint we construct a finite-mixture likelihood-ratio e-process. Ville's
inequality makes the evidence valid under continuous monitoring and arbitrary stopping.
An intersection-union rule requires every endpoint to pass. A predictable alpha-spending
ledger extends the guarantee over an unbounded, adaptively generated sequence of manuscript
versions. We prove that, conditional on the declared sampling, blinding, and log-integrity
assumptions, the probability of ever issuing a certificate to a contract-bad manuscript is
at most the ledger budget $\delta$. We also give an exact dynamic program for power, a
tamper-evident certificate schema, controls against the most economical attacks, and an
integration design for \system. The result is not a proof of beauty. It is a finite-sample
proof that a particular immutable manuscript cleared a particular reader-quality bar with a
declared maximum false-certification risk. That narrower claim is both useful and presently
implementable.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords:} long-form generation; human evaluation; e-values; confidence sequences;
sequential testing; multiple attempts; preregistration; reader behavior; LitRPG.

\section{The challenge and the change of quantifier}

\system\ is an autonomous book-production system. It already content-addresses prose,
separates generation from evaluation, preserves audit history, blocks deterministic
integrity failures, and refuses to promote an uncalibrated craft critic. Its internal
quality-measurement program has also killed twenty proposed static proxies with controls.
The transferable failures are familiar: a tricolon statistic detected publication year;
comment counts detected archive capture date; compression statistics detected sample size
or text length; model judges exhibited order artifacts; and a progression-cost heuristic
was satisfied by the bookkeeping it was meant to prevent. The local brief records the
essential rule: a metric is a number, while a quality threshold is a claim about human
judgment.

Those failures do not imply that quality is unmeasurable. They imply that an isolated scalar
computed from prose is not entitled to stand in for reader response. Human evaluation is
itself difficult: terminology varies, designs are often incompletely reported, interfaces
change judgments, and replication can fail \cite{vanderlee2021,howcroft2020,khashabi2022}.
The answer is therefore not ``ask a few people.'' It is to make the reader claim the object
of inference and to control every degree of freedom that lets the generator, evaluator, or
operator select a lucky result.

The apparent impossibility comes from a hidden universal quantifier:
\[
  \good_{\mathrm{univ}}(m)
  = ``\text{manuscript $m$ is aesthetically good for all readers and purposes}.''
\]
No finite experiment identifies this predicate. The project does not need it. Its product
goal is already narrower: books that a genre reader chooses to keep reading and recommends.
That is a claim about a population and a distribution of controlled encounters with one
manuscript. It can be false, sampled, and certified with bounded error.

This paper makes three contributions.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item It defines a \emph{quality contract} whose referent is explicit: one immutable text,
  one reader population, one exposure protocol, and a conjunction of behavior and judgment
  thresholds. It forbids scene-level evidence from silently licensing a whole-book claim.
  \item It supplies an anytime-valid test for every endpoint and a lifetime alpha ledger for
  arbitrary autonomous retries. The central theorem bounds the probability of \emph{any}
  false certificate across the entire adaptive sequence.
  \item It turns the proof into an auditable software artifact: a third party can verify the
  manuscript root, registration time, assignment transcript, outcome mapping, evidence
  path, stopping rule, and remaining error wealth without trusting the generator.
\end{enumerate}

The proposal is intentionally not an empirical victory lap. No \system\ manuscript has yet
earned this certificate. What is established here is that the certificate can exist now,
what it would prove, and exactly which failures would void it.

\section{What the certificate proves}

\subsection{The contract, not a metaphysical definition}

Let $m$ be a canonical manuscript byte string and let $h(m)$ be its Merkle root. Let
$\mathcal P$ be a named target population of readers, $\mathcal H$ a fixed distribution of
matched human comparators, and $J$ a finite set of required endpoints. A contract is
\[
 \mathcal C = \bigl(h(m),\mathcal P,\mathcal H,G,D,
       \{(Y_j,\theta_j)\}_{j=1}^{J},V,A,W\bigr).
\]
Here $G$ is the exposure grain, $D$ the trial design, $Y_j$ an executable mapping from one
reader cluster's raw event record to a binary outcome, $\theta_j$ the minimum acceptable
success probability, $V$ the assay-validity preconditions, $A$ the error ledger, and $W$ the
versioned verifier.

The manuscript root uses a canonical manifest: UTF-8 text normalized to NFC, LF line endings,
ordered chapter identifiers, byte lengths, and SHA-256 chapter digests under a domain
separator. A one-character revision creates a new root and therefore a new candidate. This
is not administrative detail. Without it, evidence collected for one text can be attached
to a later text that no reader saw.

\begin{definition}[Contract goodness]
For a valid assay $V=1$, manuscript $m$ is good under contract $\mathcal C$ when
\[
  \good_{\mathcal C}(m)
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \bigwedge_{j=1}^{J} \mu_j(m)>\theta_j,
  \qquad
  \mu_j(m)=\Prb_{\mathcal P,D}\{Y_j=1\}.
\]
It is contract-bad when at least one component satisfies $\mu_j(m)\leq\theta_j$.
\end{definition}

The strict inequality is deliberate. Equality belongs to the null and cannot earn a
certificate. The probability includes the registered sampling frame, assignment,
comparator draw, presentation order, and tie randomization. Thus the estimand is not hidden
inside prose. It is the result of a documented encounter between prose and readers.

\subsection{Three grains and no granularity laundering}

Long-form quality is not scene quality multiplied by the number of scenes. A passage can be
polished while a book fails to escalate; an exciting chapter can spend promises planted
elsewhere; a voice can drift only after 70,000 words. The contract therefore declares one of
three scopes.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{p{0.16\textwidth}p{0.29\textwidth}p{0.42\textwidth}}
\toprule
Certificate & Exposure required & Claim licensed \\
\midrule
\code{SCENE} & The entire named scene & Local readability and line-level preference only. \\
\code{ARC} & A preregistered complete arc, including setup and payoff & Dramatic function,
progression consequence, payoff, voice, and continuation across that arc. \\
\code{BOOK} & Whole-book behavior plus hidden, position-stratified arcs & The full conjunction:
reader behavior over the book and structural/craft endpoints across its length. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A \code{SCENE} certificate is not promotable to \code{BOOK} at any sample size. This mirrors
\system's existing distinction between unit-, chapter-, and story-grain calibration and
closes an ecological fallacy before statistics begin.

\subsection{Interpretation}

An accepted certificate supports exactly this sentence:

\begin{quote}
With system-wide false-certification risk at most $\delta$, this content-addressed manuscript
exceeded every preregistered threshold in contract $\mathcal C$ for readers sampled by the
declared protocol, conditional on the registered validity assumptions.
\end{quote}

It does not prove literary permanence, individual enjoyment, cross-cultural universality,
commercial success, or causal superiority under different covers, prices, platforms, and
release schedules. A narrow statement with a proof is stronger than a large statement whose
referent changes whenever challenged.

\section{The BOOK-GOOD-v1 reader contract}

This section instantiates the abstract contract for \system. Numerical thresholds must be
set by a human-only calibration study before a candidate's outcomes are observed. Values
shown as examples are design values, not measured facts.

\subsection{Target population and comparator distribution}

The population record must specify language, geography if relevant, age/consent criteria,
recent LitRPG reading behavior, subgenre familiarity, recruitment frames, device constraints,
quota weights, exclusion rules, and the calendar window during which inference is intended
to generalize. ``Online workers'' and ``readers'' are not population definitions. A useful
example is adult English-language readers who voluntarily read at least three LitRPG
chapters per week, recruited in prespecified proportions from two independent panels and a
genre-community frame.

The comparator pool is frozen separately from the candidate. It contains human-authored
LitRPG matched on subgenre tags, target length, story maturity, publication era, and content
warnings. The renderer removes title, author, cover, typography, ratings, and popularity.
Comparators are sampled by a committed rule; an operator cannot substitute a weak book after
seeing outcomes. An independent human-only calibration panel must show that the pool itself
clears an absolute reader-health bar. This prevents ``better than a bad baseline'' from being
renamed good.

Publication status is not ground truth. It is only a way to define a relevant comparison
distribution. The actual labels still come from readers in the registered trial.

\subsection{Endpoint registry}

Every primary endpoint produces one binary value per independent reader or reader block.
Ties and unavailable primary responses are handled by rules fixed in advance; they are not
deleted after inspection.

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{p{0.08\textwidth}p{0.19\textwidth}p{0.36\textwidth}p{0.25\textwidth}}
\toprule
ID & Project value & Reader-level success variable & Required comparison \\
\midrule
$Q_1$ & Revealed continuation & Reader voluntarily spends the registered extra reading
interval, reaches the next checkpoint, and does not immediately opt out. & Absolute success
above a human-calibrated floor. \\

$Q_2$ & Whole-book value & Candidate reader reaches the registered completion fraction and
recommends the book; paired comparator behavior is converted to a randomized block win. & At
least superiority to the matched human pool, or a declared noninferiority margin. \\

$Q_3$ & Dramatic function & After one hidden position-stratified arc, reader chooses the
candidate as changing goals, stakes, relationships, or available action more than the matched
arc. & Pairwise win probability above one half. \\

$Q_4$ & Progression as drama & At a progression-bearing arc, reader chooses the candidate as
making advancement more consequential and costly, rather than merely accounting for it. &
Pairwise win probability above one half. \\

$Q_5$ & Escalation and payoff & After setup and resolution are both exposed, reader chooses
the candidate as making earlier promises matter and the resolution feel earned. & Pairwise
win probability above one half. \\

$Q_6$ & Voice and line craft & Reader chooses the candidate as having the more particular,
stable narrative voice and the prose they would rather continue reading. & Pairwise win
probability above one half. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Absence of ``AI tells'' is not a separate source-detection target. Training the generator to
fool an authorship discriminator would recreate the project's failed proxy program and make
the discriminator an optimization target. Register drift, summary in place of scene,
formulaic resolution, and repetitive sentence shapes matter insofar as they reduce $Q_3$ to
$Q_6$. Post-choice source guesses are diagnostic only.

An endpoint must contain its exact prompt, renderer, response options, time window, event
parser, tie rule, missing-data rule, and aggregation code. A label such as ``engaging'' is
not executable and therefore cannot appear in a certificate.

\subsection{Why both behavior and attributed judgment are required}

Continuation alone is gameable by abrupt cliffhangers. Completion alone can reward sunk
cost. Recommendation alone is a cheap self-report. Fine-grained ratings alone invite
criterion coaching. Requiring all endpoints is a defense in depth:

\begin{itemize}
  \item $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ put opportunity cost into the outcome and observe long-range value.
  \item $Q_3$ to $Q_5$ prevent a superficial page-turner from hiding flat dramatic
  structure or consequence-free progression.
  \item $Q_6$ prevents sound plotting from excusing generic or unpleasant prose.
\end{itemize}

The conjunction is not claimed to exhaust literature. It operationalizes the priorities
already declared by this project.

\section{Trial design: readers are the experimental units}

\subsection{Panels and exposures}

\code{BOOK-GOOD-v1} uses three mutually isolated panels.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Opening panel.} A reader sees a candidate and a matched human opening in
  randomized order and makes a forced preference after equal exposure. This supplies a
  fast, cheap falsifier and an opening-level continuation measure. It cannot certify the
  book by itself.
  \item \textbf{Structural panel.} A reader is assigned one arc position from a hidden
  stratified schedule (early, middle, late, and resolution-bearing). The reader receives all
  context needed for setup and payoff, then compares it with the same functional position in
  a matched human work. Sampling positions after the manuscript is frozen prevents the
  generator from polishing only known test passages.
  \item \textbf{Long-form panel.} Readers are randomized between the candidate and a matched
  comparator in identical, source-blind reading interfaces. Checkpoint events and a final
  recommendation are logged. Compensation cannot depend on liking either text, and the
  opportunity to continue must be genuine rather than a demanded survey task.
\end{enumerate}

No reader contributes more than one primary observation to the same endpoint for a candidate.
If a person evaluates several passages for diagnostics, those records are first reduced to
one reader-level value by the registered mapping. Treating twenty scene ratings from one
reader as twenty independent readers is pseudoreplication, however small the resulting
$p$-value.

\subsection{A common Bernoulli representation}

Direct preference is binary: candidate win $=1$, comparator win $=0$. A declared tie can be
resolved by an independent fair bit from a commit-reveal seed. Counting ties as failures is
also valid but changes the estimand and loses power; dropping ties is not permitted unless
the population and threshold were defined conditionally on decisive responses.

Behavioral comparisons use randomized reader blocks. In block $i$, one fresh reader receives
the candidate and another receives a sampled human comparator. Let $B_i^C,B_i^H\in\{0,1\}$
be the registered finish-and-recommend outcomes. Define
\[
 X_i =
 \begin{cases}
  1,&B_i^C>B_i^H,\\
  0,&B_i^C<B_i^H,\\
  U_i,&B_i^C=B_i^H,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad U_i\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2),
\]
where $U_i$ is independent and auditable.

\begin{proposition}[Behavioral win identity]
If the two readers in a block are independent conditional on its registered stratum, with
success probabilities $p_C$ and $p_H$, then
\[
  \E[X_i]=\frac12+\frac12(p_C-p_H).
\]
Thus testing $\E[X_i]>1/2$ is exactly a superiority test for the behavioral success
probability. A noninferiority margin $p_C-p_H>-\varepsilon$ uses threshold
$\theta=1/2-\varepsilon/2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The candidate wins outright with probability $p_C(1-p_H)$. The outcomes tie with
probability $p_Cp_H+(1-p_C)(1-p_H)$ and then win with probability one half. Expanding and
collecting terms gives the identity.
\end{proof}

This reduction lets preference, continuation, and long-form behavior share one verified
sequential-testing implementation.

\subsection{Blinding, randomization, and controls in the same pass}

The evaluator, not the generator, owns recruitment and assignment. Candidate and comparator
are rendered through the same deterministic pipeline. Display order, comparator draw, arc
position, and tie bits use a seed whose commitment is published before the first outcome and
whose value is revealed after lock. Readers are not told authorship. Analysts see arms as
opaque labels until the primary event table is sealed.

Each run includes the confound controls most likely to fail:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Order and renderer control:} identical or human-versus-human sham pairs,
  balanced left/right, measure interface preference and order drift.
  \item \textbf{Assay sensitivity control:} an independently chosen fluent passage against
  a prespecified damaged version checks that the panel can detect a large known defect. It
  is run in a separate subpanel so it does not teach the primary criterion.
  \item \textbf{Comparator-health control:} the frozen human pool must retain its calibrated
  absolute behavior on a concurrent sample.
  \item \textbf{Identity and duplication control:} reader-cluster deduplication, delayed
  duplicate items, and reversed-order repeats quantify bots and answer inconsistency.
  \item \textbf{Attrition control:} randomized assignment counts are reconciled with event
  logs. Missing primary outcomes count as failures when ethically and legally permitted;
  otherwise a preregistered missingness cap invalidates the run.
\end{itemize}

Controls are not magic. Passing them does not prove that an online panel equals every genre
reader. They make named alternative explanations capable of killing the assay, which is the
standard the project's failed proxy program earned.

Ethics is part of validity. The protocol requires informed consent, fair compensation,
privacy-preserving reader identifiers, content warnings, withdrawal handling, and review by
the applicable ethics process. Source masking is disclosed as methodological blinding; it
must not be used to conceal risk or manipulate compensation.

\section{Anytime-valid evidence for one endpoint}

Fixed-horizon tests become invalid when an autonomous system watches them after every reader
and stops on a lucky fluctuation. Always-valid inference and confidence sequences were
developed precisely for such continuous monitoring \cite{howard2021,johari2022}. E-values
are nonnegative evidence measures with expectation at most one under the null and remain
valid under optional continuation \cite{grunwald2024}.

Fix an endpoint and suppress manuscript and endpoint indices. Let
$X_t\in\{0,1\}$ be the $t$th reader-level result, $\mathcal F_t$ the information available
after it, and $\theta\in(0,1)$ the registered quality threshold. The null is the conditional
form
\[
 H_0:\quad \E[X_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\leq\theta
 \quad\text{for every }t.
\]
For an iid probability sample this is the ordinary hypothesis $p\leq\theta$. The conditional
form also permits preregistered strata and time-varying reader mixtures whose null means
remain bounded.

Choose alternative points $q_1,\ldots,q_L\in(\theta,1)$ and fixed weights
$w_\ell>0$ summing to one. Define
\[
 M_t(q_\ell,\theta)
 =\prod_{i=1}^{t}
   \left(\frac{q_\ell}{\theta}\right)^{X_i}
   \left(\frac{1-q_\ell}{1-\theta}\right)^{1-X_i},
 \qquad
 E_t(\theta)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}w_\ell M_t(q_\ell,\theta).
\]
The finite mixture is preferred to an opaque fitted prior: every number can be stored in the
contract and recomputed with log-sum-exp arithmetic.

\begin{lemma}[Endpoint e-process]
Under $H_0$, $\{E_t(\theta)\}_{t\geq0}$ is a nonnegative supermartingale with $E_0=1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For one $q>\theta$, the next likelihood-ratio factor has conditional expectation
\[
 \E\!\left[
  \left(\frac q\theta\right)^{X_t}
  \left(\frac{1-q}{1-\theta}\right)^{1-X_t}
  \middle|\mathcal F_{t-1}\right]
 =p_t\frac q\theta+(1-p_t)\frac{1-q}{1-\theta},
\]
where $p_t=\E[X_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]$. This expression equals one at $p_t=\theta$ and has
derivative $(q-\theta)/(\theta(1-\theta))>0$ in $p_t$. It is therefore at most one whenever
$p_t\leq\theta$. Each $M_t$ is a nonnegative supermartingale. A convex combination of them
is also a nonnegative supermartingale and starts at one.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Anytime-valid endpoint certificate]
For any $\alpha\in(0,1)$ and any stopping time $T$, the rule
\[
  \text{pass endpoint if }E_T(\theta)\geq 1/\alpha
\]
has Type I error at most $\alpha$. More strongly,
\[
 \Prb_{H_0}\!\left\{\sup_{t\geq0}E_t(\theta)\geq1/\alpha\right\}\leq\alpha.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Ville's maximal inequality to the nonnegative supermartingale in the lemma
\cite{ville1939}. The event of passing at any stopping time is contained in the displayed
crossing event.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Peeking is harmless]
The trial may report its current evidence after every completed reader cluster, stop early
for success, stop for futility, or run to a maximum sample size without changing the false
pass bound. The alternative grid and outcome mapping may not be changed after outcomes are
observed.
\end{corollary}

A conventional $p$-value asks how surprising a terminal statistic would be under a fixed
plan. Here the evidence path itself is licensed for continuous use. This is essential for a
24/7 system rather than a statistical ornament.

\section{All endpoints and all autonomous manuscripts}

One valid trial does not solve autonomous search. A generator can create manuscripts until
sampling luck produces a pass. Content addressing identifies each attempt; an alpha ledger
makes the total selection risk finite.

Index manuscript attempts by $a=1,2,\ldots$. Attempt $a$ is registered with an error spend
$\alpha_a$ before its first outcome. The spend may depend on the complete history of earlier
attempts, but not on current outcomes, and must satisfy pathwise
\[
  \sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\alpha_a\leq\delta.
\]
A simple default is $\alpha_a=\delta 2^{-a}$. With lifetime risk $\delta=0.01$, the first
attempt tests at $0.005$, the second at $0.0025$, and so on. Unused budget can be handled by a
more elaborate prespecified wealth rule, but it cannot be invented after a near miss.

Within one attempt, every required endpoint is tested at level $\alpha_a$, and the manuscript
passes only when every endpoint e-process crosses $1/\alpha_a$. No Bonferroni division by
$J$ is needed. This is an intersection-union test: the bad-manuscript null is a union of
component nulls, and rejection requires rejection of every component.

\begin{assumption}[Fresh conditional validity]
Conditional on all history before attempt $a$, its manuscript, contract, and comparator rule
are fixed; its reader clusters are fresh under the registered sampling design; every
contract-bad manuscript has at least one endpoint satisfying the conditional-mean null; and
the event log, blinding, and outcome mapping have integrity.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Lifetime false-certification control]
Let $\cert_a$ denote issuance of the full certificate for attempt $a$. Under the assumption
above and a predictable ledger with total spend at most $\delta$,
\[
 \Prb\!\left\{\exists a:\ \bad_{\mathcal C_a}(m_a)
                 \ \text{and}\ \cert_a\right\}\leq\delta,
\]
even when manuscripts and future spends are chosen adaptively from all previous results.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix the history before attempt $a$ and suppose $m_a$ is contract-bad. At least one endpoint,
say $j^*$, obeys its null. Full certification implies that endpoint $j^*$ crossed
$1/\alpha_a$. By the endpoint theorem,
\[
 \Prb(\cert_a\mid\mathcal F_{a-1},\bad_a)\leq\alpha_a.
\]
This argument does not require endpoint independence and does not require knowing which
component null is true. Apply the union bound over attempts and the tower property:
\[
 \Prb(\exists a:\bad_a\cap\cert_a)
 \leq \sum_a \E\!\left[
     \ind\{\bad_a\}\Prb(\cert_a\mid\mathcal F_{a-1})
   \right]
 \leq \sum_a \E[\alpha_a]\leq\delta.
\]
Adaptive generation changes the history on which the next conditional guarantee is based;
it does not spend the same error probability twice.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the theorem does not absorb]
Optional stopping and repeated manuscripts are statistical selection problems and are
covered. Recruiting only known fans, leaking authorship, changing the renderer, duplicating
reader identities, suppressing dropouts, or modifying the manuscript after registration are
violations of the estimand or data-generating assumptions. They require prevention and audit,
not a smaller $\alpha$.
\end{remark}

\section{Power, cost, and a reproducible design calculation}

False-certification control says nothing about how many readers a genuinely good book needs.
An underpowered proof protocol is formally valid and operationally useless. This section
provides two planning calculations for a Bernoulli endpoint with threshold $\theta=0.5$ and
first-attempt level $\alpha=0.005$.

For a fixed-horizon exact binomial test, let $k_n$ be the smallest integer with
\[
  \Prb_{0.5}\{\mathrm{Bin}(n,0.5)\geq k_n\}\leq0.005.
\]
The minimum $n$ for power $\gamma$ at true success probability $p$ is the first $n$ with
$\Prb_p\{\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)\geq k_n\}\geq\gamma$.

For the anytime design, use the registered grid
\[
  q=(0.55,0.60,0.65,0.70,0.75),\qquad w_\ell=0.2.
\]
Because $E_t$ depends only on $(t,x)$, where $x$ is cumulative success, define the crossing
boundary
\[
 b_t=\min\{x:E_t(0.5;t,x)\geq200\}.
\]
Starting with $g_0(0)=1$, propagate the probability of not yet crossing:
\[
 g_t(x)=\ind\{x<b_t\}\bigl[(1-p)g_{t-1}(x)+p g_{t-1}(x-1)\bigr].
\]
Then the exact probability of certification by $n$ is $1-\sum_x g_n(x)$. No Monte Carlo is
needed. The resulting reader-level sample requirements are:

\begin{center}
\small

At $p=0.5$, the exact probability that this mixture crosses by $n=3000$ is $0.00440766$,
below the theorem's $0.005$ infinite-horizon bound. As expected, optional-stopping safety has
a sample cost. The grid is efficient when it places weight near the true effect and remains
valid when it does not.

The last column is useful for a six-endpoint contract. If every endpoint passes with
probability at least $1-0.1/6=0.9833$, the union bound guarantees at least 90\% probability
that all six pass; endpoint independence is unnecessary. In practice, outcomes have
different effects and costs, so the registered study simulator should use pilot estimates,
reader-level correlation, attrition, strata, and maximum panel capacity. The weakest
endpoint determines certification.

These numbers expose the honest price of the word ``prove.'' Hundreds of \emph{independent
reader-level outcomes} can be necessary when the true advantage is modest. Scene ratings
cannot be multiplied to make this cost disappear. A staged program controls expense:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item run controls and an opening panel as cheap falsifiers;
  \item run arc endpoints only for manuscripts that survive;
  \item reserve full-book behavioral trials and alpha spend for a release candidate;
  \item stop any endpoint for futility under a preregistered rule, which cannot create a false
  pass.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The cheapest ways to game the proof}

The project's strongest design question is: what is the cheapest repair that satisfies the
metric, and is that repair the disease? For direct reader outcomes, some optimization is the
goal. If fresh target readers consistently prefer the prose, voluntarily continue, finish,
and recommend it across structural endpoints, improving the test statistic means improving
the declared product outcome. The remaining shortcuts attack the assay rather than the book.

An autonomous generator may learn from earlier pass/fail outcomes. The lifetime theorem
allows this adaptation when every attempt uses fresh valid data and pays fresh alpha. To
reduce construct overfitting, detailed individual responses remain quarantined until the
attempt closes; later validation uses a separately recruited frame; and contract versions
rotate only on a public schedule, never in response to one manuscript. If the system learns
to satisfy actual readers under these constraints, the ``gaming'' is substantially aligned
with the construct. If it learns to infiltrate the panel, the certificate is void.

\section{A proof artifact that another machine can verify}

The paper certificate should not be a PDF badge or an evaluator's conclusion. It is a small,
signed data object plus committed evidence tables. At minimum it contains:

\begin{itemize}
  \item contract ID and digest; manuscript Merkle root and chapter manifest;
  \item population and recruitment-frame version; consent and ethics record identifiers;
  \item comparator-pool root; renderer hash; allocation and commit-reveal seed records;
  \item endpoint IDs, exact prompts, thresholds, tie and missingness rules;
  \item reader-cluster commitments, assignment transcript, raw-event Merkle root, and
  exclusion ledger;
  \item e-process grid, weights, numerical precision, evidence path, crossing time, and
  maximum sample size for every endpoint;
  \item assay-control outcomes and validity decision;
  \item lifetime ledger root, prior spends, this attempt's spend, and remaining wealth;
  \item verifier version, environment digest, timestamp, and evaluator signature.
\end{itemize}

Reader privacy does not require unverifiable statistics. The public artifact can store salted
cluster commitments and aggregate event proofs while encrypted row-level records remain with
an independent evaluator. A permitted auditor can recompute from rows; the public can verify
hash continuity, registration order, arithmetic, and signatures. Stronger zero-knowledge
machinery is possible but not required for the first implementation.

The verifier performs a fail-closed sequence:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item recompute the manuscript and comparator roots;
  \item prove that the contract and seed commitment predate all primary outcomes;
  \item validate assignments, unique reader clusters, exposures, and event reconciliation;
  \item execute each $Y_j$ mapping and reproduce the reader-level binary sequence;
  \item recompute every log e-value in registered order and its first crossing time;
  \item verify that all endpoints crossed $1/\alpha_a$ and all controls satisfy $V$;
  \item replay the alpha ledger and prove cumulative spend does not exceed $\delta$;
  \item emit the exact claim sentence, never the unqualified label ``good.''
\end{enumerate}

Any unverifiable field yields \code{INVALID}, not \code{INCONCLUSIVE} or an advisory pass.

\section{Integration with LitHarness}

The proposal fits \system's architecture but should not be squeezed into the current proxy
calibration row. The existing calibration object estimates whether a metric predicts human
judgment at unit, chapter, or story grain. The \RGC\ is direct story-grain evidence about one
immutable manuscript and a declared reader population. It belongs beside calibration as a
new artifact type.

\subsection{Suggested domain objects}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{QualityContract}: immutable hashes, population, grain, endpoints, controls,
  comparator policy, and verifier version.
  \item \code{AlphaLedger}: append-only spends keyed by manuscript root and contract family;
  it rejects a spend that would exceed lifetime wealth.
  \item \code{ReaderTrial}: external job boundary that receives opaque rendered units and
  returns signed event commitments, never generation instructions.
  \item \code{QualityCertificate}: endpoint evidence, control status, ledger proof, and exact
  licensed claim.
  \item \code{CertificateVerification}: deterministic result with reason codes and a digest
  that policy can consume.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Policy placement}

Deterministic integrity gates still run first. They are cheap necessary conditions and should
not spend readers on contradictions or broken state. Advisory craft detectors and scoped
repair remain useful for located defects, but they cannot issue the quality certificate. A
release candidate is frozen, exported through the blinded renderer, and sent to the external
reader trial. Only a verified \code{BOOK} certificate may satisfy a story-level
\code{QUALITY_BELOW_BAR} veto.

The primary reader stream is never passed to the generating model during an open attempt.
The evaluator is a separate principal with separate credentials. The generator can receive
the terminal state \code{PASS}, \code{FUTILITY}, or \code{INVALID}; any detailed feedback
used for revision becomes history, and the revision must obtain a new root and spend.

This preserves the project's existing trust posture: generation proposes; independent
evidence licenses acceptance; content address and policy record what happened.

\section{Falsification program before deployment}

A theorem conditional on bad instrumentation can certify the wrong thing perfectly. The
implementation must first try to break itself.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Arithmetic property tests.} Generate Bernoulli null streams over many seeds,
  endpoints, stopping rules, and candidate schedules. Empirical crossings should remain
  compatible with the exact boundary, while exhaustive small-state dynamic programs must
  match the verifier bit for bit.
  \item \textbf{Ledger adversary tests.} Let an agent choose the next candidate, endpoint
  correlation, stop time, and sample cap from all previous results. It should never reuse a
  spend, mutate a registered endpoint, or attach old evidence to a new root.
  \item \textbf{Renderer A/A tests.} Human work rendered through both nominal arms should show
  no arm effect. Deliberately inject author names, pagination differences, and order imbalance
  and require the control to fail.
  \item \textbf{Population replay.} Reweight the same responses under each registered frame
  and show that the certificate's claim changes when its population changes. A system that
  silently preserves the pass is mislabeling its estimand.
  \item \textbf{Pseudoreplication trap.} Feed the verifier many scene rows from a few readers.
  It must aggregate or reject them and leave the effective $n$ unchanged.
  \item \textbf{Missingness attack.} Delete unfavorable checkpoints and withdrawals. Event
  reconciliation must convert them under the fixed rule or invalidate the run.
  \item \textbf{Known-quality ladder.} Before any autonomous book can pass, run the full assay
  on source-blind strong, ordinary, and deliberately damaged human texts selected without the
  candidate. The ordering is a construct check, not a threshold-fitting opportunity.
  \item \textbf{Independent replication.} A second evaluator recruits a new frame and reruns
  the sealed contract. Disagreement does not erase the first sample; it blocks renewal and
  triggers a population or assay investigation.
\end{enumerate}

The first production certificate should be treated as a registered validation study, not a
marketing launch. Publish failures, including controls that invalidate an apparent pass.

\section{Limitations and boundaries}

\textbf{Construct validity.} Readers may continue for reasons the six endpoints do not name,
and prompts can shape judgments. The contract is defensible because it combines revealed
behavior with attributed structural comparisons, not because the list is ontologically
complete.

\textbf{External validity.} Probability sampling of genre readers is difficult. Panel
members, community volunteers, and paying customers have different incentives. The claim
must name the frame actually sampled. Replication across frames expands evidence; it does not
retroactively make the first frame universal.

\textbf{Interference and contamination.} Readers may discuss books, recognize prose, or
participate in multiple panels. Cluster identities, staggered release, and sealed text reduce
but cannot eliminate interference. Material contamination invalidates the affected attempt.

\textbf{Long-form cost.} Whole-book exposure is slow and attrition is part of the outcome.
Cheap excerpt panels are valuable falsifiers but cannot substitute for book-level behavior.
The cost is a property of the claim, not a software inefficiency.

\textbf{Composite incentives.} A fixed contract can still become culturally stale or reward
formula. Contract renewal must occur on a public calendar using human-only evidence and an
independent validation sample. It must never be tuned to rescue a current manuscript.

\textbf{Statistical proof is conditional.} The theorem proves an error bound under sampling,
mapping, and integrity assumptions. It cannot prove that recruitment vendors are honest or
that readers understood a question. Those are audited empirical premises. Calling them out
is a strength: it tells a critic exactly what must be falsified.

\textbf{No result yet.} This paper provides a method, proof, and implementation protocol. It
does not assert that an autonomous \system\ manuscript currently clears any threshold. A
certificate exists only after the registered readers have supplied the evidence.

\section{Conclusion}

The original sentence can be replaced by a more accurate one:

\begin{quote}
Autonomous long-form generation cannot prove universal aesthetic goodness, but it can
present an independently verifiable, reader-grounded proof that one immutable manuscript
exceeds preregistered quality thresholds, with a finite-sample bound on the chance of any
false certificate across continuous monitoring and adaptive retries.
\end{quote}

The method succeeds by refusing to discover goodness inside a proxy. It makes the reader
claim primary, freezes its meaning before outcomes, pays the true sample cost of long-form
evidence, and turns every optimization loophole into either an included endpoint, a measured
control, or an explicit assumption. The mathematics handles luck. Content addressing handles
version substitution. The alpha ledger handles autonomous search. Blinding and recruitment
audits handle the most dangerous confounds. The resulting certificate is narrow, expensive,
and falsifiable. Those are the properties that make it a proof rather than a score.

\section{Reference verifier pseudocode}

\small
\begin{verbatim}
verify(certificate, registry, encrypted_rows):
    require hash(certificate.contract) == certificate.contract_digest
    require merkle_root(canonicalize(manuscript)) == contract.manuscript_root
    require registry.time(contract_digest) < min(primary_outcome_times)
    require hash(reveal_seed) == registry.seed_commitment
    require comparator_root == contract.comparator_root
    require renderer_hash == contract.renderer_hash

    assignments = replay_assignments(reveal_seed, contract.design)
    require reconcile(assignments, encrypted_rows, contract.missing_rule)
    require unique_reader_clusters(encrypted_rows, contract.cluster_rule)
    require controls_pass(encrypted_rows, contract.validity_rules)

    for endpoint in contract.required_endpoints:
        x = endpoint.mapping(encrypted_rows)       # one bit per reader cluster
        require len(x) == endpoint.reported_n
        logM = [log(w) for w in endpoint.weights]
        first_crossing = NONE
        for t, bit in enumerate(x, start=1):
            for ell, q in enumerate(endpoint.q_grid):
                logM[ell] += bit * log(q / theta)
                logM[ell] += (1-bit) * log((1-q) / (1-theta))
            logE = logsumexp(logM)
            if first_crossing is NONE and logE >= log(1 / attempt_alpha):
                first_crossing = t
        require first_crossing is not NONE
        require first_crossing == endpoint.reported_crossing

    require replay_ledger(registry) <= contract.lifetime_delta
    return VERIFIED(contract.exact_claim, contract.manuscript_root)
\end{verbatim}
\normalsize

The implementation should use a documented floating-point tolerance only for display. The
authoritative comparison can use high-precision decimal arithmetic or interval bounds so a
rounding difference near $1/\alpha$ fails closed rather than selecting a pass.

\section{A worked evidence calculation}

Take $\theta=0.5$, $\alpha=0.005$, and the five-point equal-weight grid used in Section 7.
Suppose a sealed endpoint has $n=164$ reader-level results with $x=107$ successes. Its
terminal evidence is
\[
 E_{164} = \frac15\sum_{q\in\{.55,.60,.65,.70,.75\}}
       \left(\frac q{.5}\right)^{107}
       \left(\frac{1-q}{.5}\right)^{57}
       \approx 856.96.
\]
Because $856.96>1/0.005=200$, that endpoint passes. This does not mean a manuscript with
``107 of 164'' automatically earns a certificate: the path must be computed in registered
order, all other endpoints must cross, controls must pass, the alpha spend must be valid, and
the data-generating assumptions must hold. The example shows only that the arithmetic is
transparent enough to reproduce without a statistical service.
