# Paragraph revision: incidental omission delivered, opening inventory retained

Four complete edits of the actual 1924-word Chapter 1 were generated and read, with every
patch inspected against the original. Both selective edits omit some later incidental material;
neither removes the opening's information inventory. One leaves the entire first paragraph
verbatim. This workflow did not deliver the central proposed change at the opening. It does not
establish that information restructuring cannot help or that these edits improve prose quality.

## Fixed source and intervention

The original chapter is the same fixed substrate used by prose-subtraction, not a selected
candidate. Before editing, a researcher-authored map anchored 30 consequential obligations to
the source: events, relationships, knowledge, rules, quantities, purchases and endpoint. The
map is a reviewed input, not an autonomous selector or semantic instrument. It specifically
protects recognition of the removal rule's unspecified victim condition and recall's unusual
future-experience price, both vulnerable to an editor treating interpretation as disposable.

Both conditions received the same full source, map, literal spans, viewpoint and editor role.
Only the information-retention instruction differed. Both could rephrase, split and merge
paragraphs; only selective edits could omit distinct incidental information. There was no word
target, tell-count target, deletion quota, added narrative or candidate-selection call.

Source ambiguities remain deliberately unresolved: the sixty/ninety estimate, spoken contact
interpretation versus instrument wording, private awards versus the final total, and origins
of the coat and initial ranks. The editing experiment does not silently repair this canon.
