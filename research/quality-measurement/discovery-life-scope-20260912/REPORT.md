# A contributing cause in discovery's unfamiliar-life instruction

The registered contrast supports a narrow prompt cause on the two fixed sources. Every
full control adds nonhuman family or ancestry material and makes future progress depend
on helping those inhabitants. Both deletion repeats and both conditional repeats avoid
that addition on each source while retaining the supplied pursuits and power progression.
Neither assigned conditional chapter restores the addition. This meets the decision rule
in [RUNBOOK.md](RUNBOOK.md); it does not establish general originality or better prose.

The correction makes the elaboration conditional on the author's brief introducing unfamiliar
life or intelligence. It neither prohibits new beings nor adds a forbidden-topic list.
Water, administrative conflict and human repair work remain in outputs. The larger repetition
problem, including initial invention before discovery planning, remains unresolved.

## Provenance and controls

> Give unfamiliar life or intelligence its own pursuits, relationships and history, with tangible traces inviting contact and investigation.

The omitted arm deletes exactly that sentence. The scoped arm substitutes:

> If the author's brief introduces unfamiliar life or intelligence, develop its own pursuits, relationships and history, with tangible traces inviting contact and investigation.

The three systems contain 1964, 1825 and 2001 characters respectively. User prompts, schemas,

The conditional chapters satisfy the registered no-restoration check while preserving the
stories. Both full chapters also omit their plans' later family dependencies. Consequently,
the chapter comparison does **not** demonstrate removal of an enacted subplot or superior
chapter quality. It only checks that the corrected plans can yield these openings without
restoring the targeted addition. One chapter per assigned plan and unblinded inspection do
not qualify a general reader instrument or establish long-book behavior.
