# Source selection between planning and writing: four connected diagnostics

Registered for the operator's 2026-09-06 request to test an intermediary agent and the preceding
source-exposure, scene-type, language and subscription-writer suggestions. These are diagnostic
conjectures, not quality metrics, candidate selection, production changes or a narrative template.
The previous narration-obligations arm still supplied all 42 units; both selective drafts
narrated all 11 optional units. The framing arm already tested a different context selector:
this is a narrower source-boundary comparison, not a claim that selection is a new idea.

## A. Actual source withholding and an intermediary

Use the same reviewed 42-unit Chapter 1 map. Keep its 31 mandatory units, all literal occurrences,
consequential rules, physical events and endpoint. Three conditions have the same system and
31 required-narration IDs: full (42 units visible), static (only the 31 visible), editor (31 plus
an agent-selected subset of the other 11). The full condition corresponds to the earlier
selective obligation, not that experiment's all-required control. Original prose stays audit-only.

One Codex/high intermediary receives the full map and fixed mandatory/optional classification.
It decides KEEP/DROP for each optional ID and gives a short reason. It cannot rewrite, invent,
remove mandatory units, draft, rank or assess candidate prose. Only unchanged retained units
reach the writer, in source order; reasons do not. All/none optional retained is valid and is
reported even if it duplicates another input. Two Codex/high drafts per condition, no selection.
The intermediary's one decision set is shared by both draws: those are not independent editors.

Pre-call source changes, shared across all three: put the scene break after mandatory F14a,
replace its reference to withheld F13 with an event-relative time, and replace global notes
that leak optional estimates with generic ambiguity preservation. The exact changes and source
review remain local. These changes mean a fresh full control is necessary. Do not remove an
optional fact yet reintroduce its contents in metadata, instructions or editor rationale.

Withholding known mental outcomes may change the story's information, not only prose. An
unstated optional inference is not proof of preserved thought or reader inference. Audit all
42 units against the original, distinguishing omitted optional information, missing required
information, located implication, contradiction and uncertain rendering. Do not credit loss
of physical causes or required interiority as stylistic improvement.

## B. Mechanical obstacle versus conflicting intentions

Four opening scenes from two local, author-created fixtures: a mechanical impediment with a
cooperative student, and a student's refusal motivated by an incompatible immediate goal.
Share viewpoint, examination setting, collection task, clock pressure and handover endpoint.
Allow dialogue and immediate interaction in both, without a detailed beat outline or word
target. Two Codex/high draws each in mechanical-1, social-1, social-2, mechanical-2 order.

This is an alternative ordinary-exam opening, not a rewrite of the supernatural Chapter 1 or
a continuity-preserving replacement. The obstacle and its causal facts differ; the scene is
shorter in scope and loses the original's speculative-rule burden. It is a transfer diagnostic
of how intentions are rendered, not an isolated cause-of-prose experiment. No fixture enters
production. Read both full scenes, not just dialogue or first paragraphs. Locate explanation
of motives already apparent in interaction, unearned resolution, inventions and ending morals.

## C. Russian drafting followed by English rendering

Two Russian-to-English chains and two English-to-English chains, four calls each, Codex/high.
All first stages use the fixed 31-unit source regardless of A's outcome. The only first-stage
instruction difference is the language of ordinary narration/dialogue. Protected English
literal spans stay English in both. Both second stages use exactly the same rendering system,
receiving only their own intermediate passage and common protected literals. They do not see
the original source, sibling prose, commentary or a preferred candidate. No repair opportunity
after inspecting an intermediate. Retain and read all eight passages, including Russian.

Order: Russian source-1/render-1, English source-1/render-1, English source-2/render-2,
Russian source-2/render-2. The English rewrite controls for a second pass, not every effect
of language, translation length or source-language competence. Record changed/added/lost
meanings across each chain and against the full source. English regex counters do not apply
to Russian originals. Any cadence change bought by meaning loss remains a trade.

Claude static-1; Codex editor-1, editor-2; Codex static-2, Claude static-2; Codex full-2,

separately reported reasoning token dispatch stop. Codex cached input is already included;
Claude uncached input, cache-read and cache-creation tokens are summed into input.
