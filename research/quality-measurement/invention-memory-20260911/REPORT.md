# Plot-history instructions change some premises; expansion still recurs

Explicit contrast with previous initiating actions and plot sequences is a feasible lead in
these draws. The structural arm produced deliberate counterattacks, tracking hunts, bodily
transformations and component-building pursuits alongside familiar institutional conflicts.
Ordinary history also produced departures, so the added sentence does not get all the credit.
Carrying history and a premise-preservation instruction into expansion kept one opening's
invention-and-flight progression more directly intact, but another still added nurseries and
infrastructure politics. The combined intervention is not a sufficient repetition solution.

Preselection chose one-based positions 5 and 2, shared across the three arms within each

third person, with no writer or additional prefix. Two further expansions used the exact
same structural-arm premises with history, structural contrast and an explicit instruction
to preserve the premise's central action and pursuit. Their order was reversed across blocks.

## What changed at invention

The no-history draws reproduce several concrete patterns already visible in earlier runs:
professional reality manipulation rescues a settlement and harms another, restorative
craft threatens the structures supporting civilization, and food or maintenance powers
develop into contested community services. Both baseline first items are surveyors whose
territorial intervention helps a settlement and creates damage elsewhere. Their later
conflicts differ; they are not identical stories.

The identity-history arm changes names, powers and some pursuits. It nevertheless often
starts with rescuing somebody, then develops the consequences into obligations or conflict
with an institution. Examples include the dream researcher moving a threat into another
patient's dream and the star inventor saving a caravan while taking royal stars. This is
not its entire repertoire: the fencer who buys a movement restriction and the octopus
wrestler pursuing a shrine are counterexamples to a uniform rescue-to-service description.

The structural arm contains different concrete sequences: accepting an injury to acquire
a counterattack, following a physical trail to attack a predator, folding a shadow body to
escape, and iteratively assembling machines that attract rewarded hunters. Those differences
involve action and continuation, beyond renamed occupations or changed scenery. Some still
lead to rebellion, governance or rescue. In particular, the selected electrician already
has a network-control conflict in the short premise; its presence in expansion is not wholly
a downstream invention.

The twelve structural premises do not establish independence from one another or from all
prior material. Constellation powers occur in identity-2 item 2 and structure-1 item 2;
circuits and combined devices recur across both history arms; dreams appear in three new
batches; combat restrictions and tournaments form another broad family. These pairs also
have different actions, and sharing a broad mechanism alone does not make them duplicates.
The swimmer in structure-1 still manifests water. Its mere presence is not a failure label.

The complete inventory records every premise, including these counterexamples. No model
scored or ranked them, and this inspection supplies no quality label or novelty percentage.

## What happened to all eight selected plans

| Expansion | Central action and pursuit | Added material and continuation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| baseline-1: cook | Feeding an injured monster creates a peaceful sanctuary contested by combat progression. | Adds pupil evacuation, an inherited orchard, pollinator nurseries and collective public-work contracts. The rescue and service trajectory was already in the premise. |
| identity-1: dream researcher | Saves a drowning soldier by changing a dream rule; displaced danger leads toward linked refuges. | Develops another patient's flooding, a courier's delivery obligations, restored routes and sleeping workers' shared shelters. Ordinary history has not prevented the familiar trajectory. |
| structure-1: electrician, clean | Uses a wand and wired auction benches to incapacitate captors; develops programmable rebel weapons. | Adds a backfeed that locks the exit, a repairer's escape assistance, mineral people's ancestral chambers and a dispute over essential services and network ownership. |
| structure-1: electrician, retained | Keeps the same offensive escape and rebel engineering pursuit. | Disconnects the incoming supply before attacking, but still adds mineral people's communal nurseries, stolen nursery branches, repaired mains and local control of infrastructure. |
| structure-2: inventory inventor, clean | Builds an aircraft from adjacent objects while seeking a transmitter for his offshore daughter. | Adds flooded suburbs and rescuing the facility manager across water; that rescue earns the next inventory capability. Ceramic households and negotiated machine protection follow. |
| structure-2: inventory inventor, retained | Builds the same aircraft and pursues the same transmitter. | Adds steering experiments and a flight onto the roof; his own successful prototype use earns the next capability. Ceramic repairers, missing-family obligations and cooperation with machine hunters remain. |
| identity-2: star inventor | Catches a meteor to save a caravan and claims royal stars. | Develops water transport and retrieval powers, repaired observatory instruments, displaced creature families and negotiated access to shared stars. The rescue and ownership conflict remain central. |
| baseline-2: worker bee | Distributes a queen's fatal wound across workers, creating a survival crisis. | Adds nursery relocation, ancestral brood comb, beetle trade agreements and rules governing consent and communal advancement. These support rather than replace the original hive conflict. |

The inventory inventor supplies the clearest limited lead. With the same source premise,
the clean expansion inserts a neighbor rescue as the successful power use that earns growth;
the retained version makes testing and flying the invention that event. Neither loses the
transmitter or daughter. Offshore travel was already in the premise, so removing all water
would not be a sensible preservation requirement. The observable difference is the event
driving opening advancement, not a water-word count.

The electrician provides the counterexample to sufficiency. Both expansions preserve the
auction escape and weapons craft, while nursery/ancestral care, maintained service networks
and their governance surround the action again. The retained inventor plan also keeps
ceramic repairer families and reciprocal help. Carry-through has not removed the recurrent
surrounding repertoire, and this pilot ends before world construction or chapter drafting.

## Decision and next discriminating control

Keep this as a limited feasibility observation. Two blocks sharing one history do not
establish a general benefit or an average effect size. Identity already changes some
sequences. The structural instruction is longer, and both history conditions have much
more context than baseline. Requested transport controls cannot exclude backend variation.
These results identify neither training-distribution distance nor the root cause of repetition.

The retained expansion combines three things: prior stories, structural contrast, and a
positive instruction to preserve the supplied premise. The inventor's changed opening
cannot be attributed to memory alone. The next useful control is **preservation only**:
use the same fixed premises and renderer with that positive instruction but no history,
alongside clean and combined versions in a newly registered balanced sequence. It has not
been run here. It would test whether a small handoff requirement can retain the initial
causal sequence without carrying an expanding archive of previous plots.

The current CLI's prior-concept projection intentionally uses `render_for_world`, which
omits discovery opening and growth, and `distinct_from` applies only to first invention.
These are verifiable design boundaries, not demonstrated bugs. A production plot projection
or persistence change needs its own bounded test and a deliberate contract change. This
pilot changes no production prompt, selector, default or database. The requested opaque
Base64 default remains in place.

The literature record retains other leads and their limitations; none supplies a validated
LitHarness solution. No full books, reader responses or literary quality were measured.
