# History coverage during expansion

The operator requested continued solution search after the verbalized-sampling pilot.
The earlier history experiment supplied short premises even during expansion. Repeated
nursery, ancestral-site and repair-bargain material was often added only in expanded plans.
This pilot tests whether including those complete plans changes the added repertoire.
It isolates history coverage from an extra preservation instruction, not from context size.

## Registered inputs and conditions

Use all eight previously preselected stories from invention-verbalized-20260911, in that
experiment's invention order. For each, retain its exact selected premise and its one
unchanged-discovery expansion. The eight full histories are objects with premise, world,
opening and growth. The eight short histories contain only the same premise fields, in
the same order. Use all records, without content selection, compression or model summary.
Freeze source receipts, their previous evidence hashes, selection lineage and individual
text hashes. This protocol authorizes these generated texts as negative reference data
only inside this isolated experiment. No probabilities, reports, diagnoses, reader answers,
corpus excerpts, literature examples or inspection notes enter generation.

Generate two new batches of six premises using the short history and a common instruction
to differ in protagonists, settings, powers, actions, causal sequences, pursuits and conflicts.
Keep the previous 80-120-word task and schema. Draw two independent 2048-bit integers for
the default v3 Base64 prefixes, plus separate selection integers before any response.
The registered PRNG selects one of six positions per batch uniformly, independent of
response contents. Do not redraw or replace an item if it is familiar or inconvenient.
Retain all twelve premises. There is no invention-stage treatment comparison in this pilot.

For each selected premise compare three expansion conditions, with two fresh-session repeats:

- clean: unchanged discovery.render_request, third person, no writer or history.
- short: the clean request plus the eight short records as previous territory and the
  expansion contrast instruction frozen in run.py.
- full: exactly the same instruction, envelope and record order as short, with world,
  opening and growth added to each prior record.

Both history conditions ask for different added actions, causal sequences, pursuits,
conflicts and world material. Neither adds the separate preservation sentence or the old
combined CARRY instruction. Both receive the exact current premise after the history.
There is no extra opaque prefix during expansion. Short versus full changes the available
details and the context length; it does not isolate meaning from length or establish an
optimal history size. Clean versus short bundles reference context and novelty wording.

Generate invent-1 then invent-2. The expansion order is:

1. Premise 1, repeat 1: clean, short, full.
2. Premise 2, repeat 1: short, full, clean.
3. Premise 1, repeat 2: full, short, clean.
4. Premise 2, repeat 2: clean, full, short.

The second repeat reverses the first ordering within each premise. Request bytes must be
identical across repeats of a premise and condition. The two selected premises are source
units; repetitions and the six items in a batch are not independent sources. One common
history limits transfer claims. No model ranks, scores, rejects or chooses candidates.

## Inspection and decision rule

Read no newly generated prose until all slots finish or an operational stop fires.
Audit all first responses, frozen source and binary, prepared and captured requests,
native effective system including the schema instruction, history coverage and provenance,
repeat equality, usage and sessions. Keep all outputs and structural failures. An invalid
invention response skips only its six dependent expansions, with no substitute.

Read all twelve new premises and all completed plans. Record bounded paraphrases tied to
hashes of the initiating action, consequential power use, advancement, continuing pursuit,
invented inhabitants and their added conflicts. Examine the recurring rescue-to-obligation
pattern and care sites, inherited infrastructure and repair bargains around it. Also record
departures, source-preservation failures and new repeated families. Word counts and literal
matches describe artifacts; they are not novelty/quality scores. A water-free setting,
renamed occupation or unfamiliar body is not sufficient evidence of a new causal structure.

Full-history coverage is a feasibility lead only if its added material departs beyond both
short and clean controls across both repeats and both selected premises while retaining
the supplied stories. If full still reconstructs the familiar structure, simply renames
its inhabitants, replaces the supplied pursuit or converges on another repeated family,
it is insufficient here. A single favorable plan or a difference also present in the
controls does not meet this rule. If short already performs comparably, do not credit the
additional complete plans. If fresh source premises themselves remain repetitive, report
that limitation and distinguish faithful expansion from newly introduced recurrence.

This is an unblinded qualitative artifact inspection, not a qualified literary measure,
a population novelty rate, training-distribution distance, general history refutation or
proof of root cause. Missing outputs make their comparisons incomplete. No chapters or
full books are generated, and no production feedback or default change follows this pilot.
