# Full-plan history coverage: observed, insufficient in this pilot

Supplying all eight previous plans did not meet the registered feasibility criterion.
Both full-history expansions of Dev still add mineral inhabitants' breeding sites and
cooperation needed to develop his digestion. Ruth's full-history expansions retain
repair-for-passage and migration disputes; some of that repetition already belongs to
her supplied premise. Departures also appear in the shorter controls. The longer history
therefore has no consistent advantage across both premises and both repeats.

All fourteen first attempts completed and passed structural validation. This is a bounded,
unblinded reading of saved artifacts, not a literary-quality or population-novelty measure.
The claim remains **OBSERVED**. No production default changes follow.

## Decision and next lead

The test closes the narrow coverage gap: the recurring expanded material was present in
the full negative-reference context, yet it still appeared. This does not show that the
model ignored history, that history caused repetition, or that every history method fails.
The earlier short records already included some nursery and egg material too. The result
does not warrant growing a production archive of full plans as the solution.

The unchanged discovery system asks for unfamiliar intelligence with pursuits,
relationships and history, plus a next capability grounded in an opening encounter. Those
instructions are observable; whether their combination causes the repeated nursery and
reciprocal-service realization remains untested here. The controls hold that system fixed.
Both source premises also bring ecological conflict before expansion begins, and there
are only two source units, one common history and two repeats per condition. The absence
of an invention control, context-length confounding, unblinded interpretation and unknown
backend behavior limit every causal claim. No chapters, books or reader evaluation were run.

A candidate next experiment is active use of the opaque prefix at invention. The primary
[String Seed of Thought paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21150v1), section 3 and Appendix A,
asks a model to generate a random string and actively use its contents in producing a
response. Its open-ended prompt includes an explicit reasoning stage. That is different
from merely prepending an unexplained string. A LitHarness adaptation would keep the
operator's external number-to-Base64 seed and final story schema, testing an active-use
instruction against passive-prefix and matched wording controls. It would not reproduce
the paper's full prompt, and the paper supplies no validation for Astra LitRPG stories.
This is a **CONJECTURE**, not a result of the present pilot or an actual sampling control.

The paper is already content-addressed in the earlier [source record](../invention-memory-20260911/sources.json):
`runs/invention-memory-20260911/sources/2510.21150v1.html`, SHA-256
`ed08dfd5c7e192dc3252b0004d047f0aeb8ce1a08986300c93f741a111d731ae`.
It was revisited during the current run; it was not a treatment source or generation input.
A future registration must fix its controls and decision rule before calls, retain all
outputs and use externally fixed positions without model ranking or authored world palettes.
