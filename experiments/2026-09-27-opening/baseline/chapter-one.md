# Every Skill a Handhold

## Chapter 1

Owen had pulled every tin to the front of the shelf except the ones behind his phone.

On the screen, a climber hung sideways beneath a plywood overhang. Owen paused the video before the move he kept getting wrong. Left foot higher. Turn the knee in. Stop trying to haul himself up by his arms.

“Owen.”

He put the phone against his thigh.

Leanne stood at the end of the aisle with an empty stock trolley. His night-shift supervisor glanced from his hand to the untouched cardboard tray beside his shoes.

“Are those going to face themselves?”

“Nearly done.”

He was twenty-four, and he still said it in the voice he’d used when his mum asked about homework.

Beyond Leanne, the main aisle ran towards the tills and the glass frontage. In the other direction, past household cleaning, a doorway opened into the rear loading corridor. Dev was back there with the floor scrubber. Its steady whine had been following Owen around the shop all night.

He wanted that sound to stop, the shift to end, and the climbing wall to open before everyone with a proper job arrived.

His phone went white.

Not bright: letters. Still white letters on a charcoal background.

EVERY PERSON HAS RECEIVED ONE SLOT.
CHOOSE WHAT YOU WILL BE ABLE TO DO.

The same message stood across his sight when he looked up.

Owen blinked hard. The words remained in front of the baked beans.

“Leanne?”

“I can see it.”

At the far end of the shop, every lit till screen carried the message. Leanne lifted her own phone, then lowered it without touching anything.

Something cracked behind the cleaning aisle.

The scrubber stopped.

A second crack went through the floor beneath Owen’s shoes. It sounded like a pallet breaking under too much weight, except it kept going. Leanne caught the trolley as its wheels rattled.

From the rear doorway came a rising scrape of metal.

“Dev?” she called.

Owen stepped into the main aisle.

The floor of the loading corridor buckled. Pale tiles tilted towards each other, then burst apart around a dark, rounded mass. A root pushed through, thick as the scrubber’s body, carrying wet earth and scraps of something blue.

It struck the doorframe.

Owen backed away so quickly that he collided with Leanne’s trolley.

“Front doors,” she said. “Go.”

Another root dragged across the doorway. Behind it, a fluorescent fitting swung down on its cable and struck the wall.

There should have been a staff car park beyond that wall.

Through a widening split above the corridor, Owen saw leaves.

They were hanging against a cliff face, far higher than the shop roof. Water spilled from a stone channel through the break, warm drops striking his cheek. Above it were narrow ledges planted with small trees. A light moved behind their branches.

For a moment he could not make his feet follow Leanne.

Then the ceiling cracked, and he followed.

They reached the next aisle before a new panel opened in his sight. This one had his name.

Owen Price
SLOT COUNT: 1
SLOT CAPACITY: UNLIMITED
INSTALLED SKILLS: NONE
SLOT RANK: 1

He stopped beside a display of washing powder.

His own name made it worse. The message on the tills might have been something happening to the shop. This was happening to him.

Leanne was ahead of him, turning back.

“Keep moving.”

“What does yours say?”

“What?”

“Capacity.”

“One.” Her gaze shifted across something he couldn’t see. “One Slot. Nothing installed. Why?”

“Mine says unlimited.”

She looked at him properly then.

“Unlimited what?”

“I don’t know.”

He tried to dismiss the sheet as though closing an advert. It faded when he wanted it gone. That small obedience frightened him almost as much as the roots.

The route to the frontage was clear. Beyond the tills, the automatic doors stood open, and he could see the pavement under the streetlights. Nothing had broken there. He could get outside. He could phone someone, if phones still did that.

“Owen!”

Dev’s voice came from the rear.

Owen turned.

“Don’t leave me back here.”

Leanne had heard. She pointed towards the doors.

“I’ll check outside. Don’t get under that ceiling.”

He nodded, already going the other way.

At the loading doorway, he stopped well short of the broken tiles.

A root had lifted the corridor floor into a ridge. Beyond it, the floor had fallen away altogether, leaving a dark gap between Owen and the raised concrete landing at the staff exit. Dev crouched on that landing beside the scrubber. The machine had tipped against the exit door.

“Can you shift it?” Owen called.

“Tried. Door won’t move anyway.”

Dev held one arm across his middle. There was blood on his sleeve.

Along the left side of the corridor, the painted block wall still joined doorway to landing. Owen traced it automatically: start low, move sideways, keep his weight over his feet.

There were no holds.

No projecting bricks, no pipe he could trust. Just smooth paint above the torn edge of the floor. At the climbing wall, he would have laughed at someone calling it a route.

A quiet choice appeared when he thought of the Slot.

CLING, Controlled adhesion through hands and feet.
ANCHOR, Hold your own position against external force.
KINDLE, Ignite suitable material by touch.

Owen read the first line again.

He put his palm against the corridor wall. Ordinary paint. Ordinary skin. When he leaned, his hand slid.

Across the gap, Dev watched him.

“Can you get over?”

Owen looked at the word Cling, and his mouth went dry.

“I might be able to.”

* * *

Anchor sounded safer. Hold your position. Stay where you were while everything else went wrong.

But Dev was on the other side of the break. Staying here would not help him, and Owen could see nothing useful to set on fire.

He chose Cling before he could talk himself out of it.

[STATUS] Owen Price — Cling 1

The other choices vanished.

His palm remained against the painted blocks. Nothing tingled. He could still feel grit beneath his skin and the cool wall beneath that.

Then he tried to slide his hand.

It stayed.

Owen pulled back sharply. His palm released as soon as he wanted it to, leaving him staring at his fingers.

“What happened?” Dev called.

“I took Cling.”

“Does it work?”

He pressed his hand flat again and leaned away. The skin across his palm tightened. His wrist took the pull.

“Yes.” He swallowed. “I think so.”

Footsteps stopped behind him. Leanne had returned to the loading doorway, keeping clear of the broken tiles.

“Pavement’s clear outside,” she said. “What are you doing?”

“Going along the wall.”

Her eyes followed the blank paint to Dev’s landing.

“With what?”

“This.”

He lifted a foot and pressed the sole against the wall. It held when he put weight through it. Not on a ledge, not against a lump in the paint. Just there.

For an instant he wanted Leanne to see exactly how impossible that was.

Then a tile dropped from the broken floor and he heard it hit something far below.

“Can you do the same?” Owen asked Dev.

“Already picked Anchor. When the floor started moving.”

Dev shifted his free hand from the landing, then planted it again.

“It’s keeping me here.”

“Right. Stay there.”

As instructions went, it was useless. Dev nodded anyway.

Owen put both hands against the wall and brought his other foot up. Now nothing beneath him belonged to the floor.

His stomach lurched. He kept his eyes on the paint.

At the climbing wall, starting a traverse was something he did while talking. Here he spent too long checking each contact, until his shoulders began to ache before he had moved beyond the doorway.

Feet first. Stand on them.

He edged sideways, releasing one shoe and placing it farther along. The adhesion returned when he wanted it. His body seemed to understand that much. It did not understand where to put the rest of him.

He reached too far with his right hand.

His hips swung away from the wall. Weight dragged through his outstretched arm, and pain caught at the base of his wrist.

“Owen,” Leanne said.

“I know.”

He didn’t, for a moment. His left foot was too far behind him, his right knee bent beneath his chest. He tried to pull himself closer and made the pain worse.

Stop hauling.

He brought the trailing foot underneath his hip. Turned the knee out. Pushed through the leg until his body rose and the load eased off his hand.

There.

He stayed still long enough to breathe.

“You all right?” Leanne asked.

“Keep talking to Dev.”

She did. Asked where he was hurt, whether he could feel his foot. Owen listened without trying to answer. A leg injury. Something sharp when the floor broke. Yes, Dev could move his toes.

The landing came closer in short, careful movements. Owen kept his arms almost straight and his steps small. He wanted to look down each time loose grit fell beneath his shoes. He looked instead at the next place his hand would go.

At last he could put a foot on concrete.

He tested it, then stepped onto the landing beside Dev and released the wall.

[STATUS] Owen Price — Slot Rank 2

The words caught his eye as his weight left his hands.

Two. He had been one before.

There was no rush of strength. His wrist still hurt. But when he steadied himself against the wall, the grip felt less precarious, as though he no longer had to hold the intention quite so tightly.

“You actually did it,” Dev said.

Owen crouched beside him.

Blood had soaked the trouser leg below Dev’s knee. His sleeve was wet where he had pressed it against the wound. Owen could see a cut through the torn fabric, but not how deep it went.

“Can you keep pressure on that?”

“Been trying.”

“I know. Sorry.”

A metal maintenance rack stood between the scrubber and the side wall. Owen had to turn sideways to reach it. Beneath bottles and a battered toolbox lay a coil of braided rope, dirty at the loose end but apparently whole.

He lifted it and worked the nearest length through his hands. No obvious cuts. That was all he knew how to check.

“I’ve got rope,” he called.

“Bring an end back,” Leanne said. “I’ve taken Anchor. There’s an upright by the doorway we can use as well.”

Owen looked across. She stood inside the shop with one hand around the steel upright, away from the fractured edge.

He pictured Dev trying to follow his route with an injured leg and no grip on the wall.

“Don’t just hold him yourself,” he said.

“I wasn’t planning to.”

He nodded, embarrassed and grateful at once.

With the coil over his shoulder, he moved to the edge of the landing. His hands were shaking now. He flexed them, watching the wall he had crossed.

A crack ran through the paint.

Owen stepped back.

The blocks in the middle of his route bulged inward. Mortar spilled into the gap. Then a whole section broke loose and fell, taking his imagined footholds with it.

Dust struck his face.

When it cleared, the wall ended short of his reach. Beyond the missing section, Leanne still stood by the upright.

Owen gripped the rope.

He had brought it as far as Dev. It was still on the wrong side.

* * *

“Back from the edge,” Leanne called.

Owen moved until his shoulder touched the tipped scrubber. Dev sat between it and the wall, pressing his sleeve against his leg. The rope hung heavy on Owen’s shoulder.

The missing wall opened onto wet stone and tangled roots. The surviving section on Leanne’s side looked horribly close. Close enough to imagine catching; too far to reach.

Something pale knocked against the landing.

A slender root lay across the broken concrete beside the scrubber. Wedged around a fork in it was a ceramic sleeve, blue beneath the dust. Inside sat a translucent seed with a branching pattern suspended in its centre.

Owen crouched. The sleeve had a smooth rim and little holes spaced around its base. Someone had made it.

“Careful,” Dev said.

“I’m just looking.”

He touched the seed.

AIR STEP
A brief foothold on empty air.
Recover your breath before reuse.
INSTALL?

His hand stopped.

Beyond the broken wall, there was nowhere to put the foot that would push him across. Now the words offered exactly that.

“What is it?” Dev asked.

“A skill. Air Step.”

“Instead of sticking?”

Owen looked at the wall he needed to catch.

If Cling went away halfway across, a foothold would only let him jump into the remaining blocks before falling. He brought his sheet into view. One Slot. Unlimited capacity. Cling still listed beneath installed skills.

Unlimited had looked like a mistake beside the washing powder.

He wanted it to mean something now.

“Mine might keep both,” he said.

“Might?”

There wasn’t a better answer.

Owen chose to install.

The seed collapsed beneath his fingertip into a faint dust that disappeared before it reached the ceramic rim. The empty sleeve stayed on the root.

[STATUS] Owen Price — Air Step 1

Cling remained on his sheet beside Air Step. His rank had not changed.

He pressed his palm to the wall and pulled gently away. His skin tightened; his hand held.

“Still got it,” he said.

Dev let out a breath.

Owen wished that were enough.

He put the coil down and fed a length towards Dev, leaving plenty loose on the landing. He would carry the end rather than risk the whole coil catching on the broken wall.

“Don’t tie it to yourself,” he said. “Let it run after me.”

“You’re going to jump?”

“Not from here. I can get closer along what’s left.”

Leanne watched from beside the upright across the gap. He told her what the seed had offered. Saying it aloud made the plan sound worse.

“Can you get back if it doesn’t work?” she asked.

“Here, yes.”

He turned to Dev. “Are you still holding yourself?”

Dev nodded, his good hand flat on the concrete. “Go on.”

Owen kept one shoe on the landing and fixed both palms to the wall. He extended his other foot over the gap.

The place for it came as naturally as the intention to release Cling. There was resistance beneath his sole, flat and firm.

He hesitated.

When he finally pushed down, the support was gone.

His foot dropped. His hands caught his weight, and pain flared through the wrist he had already strained. He dragged his knee back onto the landing.

“Owen?”

“Stayed here,” he managed.

He bent over his knee. His chest felt as though he had just finished a hard problem at the climbing wall. When he tried to make another foothold, nothing met his shoe.

Recover your breath.

He waited, furious with himself for spending strength on a step he hadn’t taken.

It had felt solid. That was the useful part. He had waited for proof instead of moving, and used up the moment in which it could help him.

He shook out one hand, then the other.

“Don’t wait on it,” he said.

“What?” Dev asked.

“Me. Talking to me.”

His breathing eased. Keeping his hands low and his weight over the landing, he tried again. This time he placed the foot and pressed immediately.

His body rose. He brought his knee onto the concrete before the foothold vanished.

Not a staircase. One push.

Across the gap, Leanne lowered herself beside the upright, ready for the rope.

Owen tucked the rope end through his belt loosely enough to pull free. Then he climbed onto the remaining wall.

Feet close beneath his hips. Arms long. He knew these things. Fear kept trying to make him forget them.

At the broken edge, he looked once at the landing behind him. Dev had the loose rope clear of the scrubber.

Owen recovered his breath.

He needed to leave the wall before he could push towards the far section. For a moment there would be nothing holding him.

He released.

His stomach lifted. He placed his foot into emptiness and drove through the brief resistance beneath it.

The far wall struck his palms harder than he expected. Cling caught. His body swung below his hands, rope dragging at his belt.

Feet.

He slapped one sole against the blocks, brought the other underneath him and pushed his hips towards the wall.

[STATUS] Owen Price — Slot Rank 3

His grip steadied. The pain in his wrist did not disappear.

Both. He had used both.

“Owen, come down.”

Leanne’s voice drew his eyes to the solid floor beneath her. He traversed the last stretch and stepped down beside the upright.

She caught his elbow. Only when she let go did he realise he was shaking.

They passed the rope around the steel. Owen made close turns and secured the end while Leanne checked that each turn sat against the upright rather than broken masonry. She took the loaded length with the steel between her and the gap, planted her feet and used Anchor.

“Slowly,” she called to Dev. “Pull against it first.”

The rope straightened. Nothing shifted at their end.

Dev could now pull towards them without asking an injured leg to find an invisible foothold. They still had to get him off the landing.

Owen looked at the line, then at his aching hands.

“Right,” Leanne said. “How do we support his leg?”

* * *

“Something under his leg,” Owen said. “Not just a rope round his middle.”

Across the gap, Dev looked down at the line in his hands.

“I’d quite like something under the rest of me.”

The joke made Owen breathe out. He had been staring at the drop again.

There were folded decorators’ sheets on the maintenance rack. Dev could reach them without leaving the landing. With Leanne calling instructions, he dragged one over, folded it into a broad strip and worked it beneath his thighs. His face tightened when he moved the injured leg.

Owen watched him struggle to feed rope beneath the cloth.

“I need to go back.”

Leanne looked at his hands.

“To help him tie it,” he said. “Then I come back before you pull.”

“Can you?”

He wanted to say yes quickly.

“After a minute.”

She let him have it.

The return crossing was no less frightening for knowing it could work. He waited until his breathing settled, climbed to the broken edge and committed his weight to the air foothold without testing it first. His palms caught the surviving wall. He brought his feet up before his hips could swing far.

On the landing, he rested his forehead against the blocks.

“You don’t have to look pleased about it,” Dev said.

“Wasn’t planning to.”

They made a padded seat from the folded sheet, held by broad rope loops beneath Dev’s thighs and behind his back. Owen knew knots from the climbing wall, but this was not a harness. He checked every knot, then had Dev lean into it while the concrete still supported him.

The rope tightened towards Leanne’s upright.

“Anything slipping?” she called.

“No,” Dev said. “Digging in, though.”

They adjusted the cloth. Owen folded another sheet beneath Dev’s injured calf and secured it to the seat so the lower leg would not hang loose. Dev could still press his bloodied sleeve against the wound.

Owen looked at what they had made. He wanted proper equipment, someone qualified, a floor.

“We’ll keep you as upright as we can,” he said.

“Just get me over.”

Owen returned along the wall and stopped on its last intact section beside the shop floor. Leanne took up the slack around the upright. From there the rope would draw Dev towards them, but it would also lower him below the landing before they could haul him up.

They told him that. Dev closed his eyes briefly, then nodded.

“Let your Anchor go when you’re ready,” Leanne called.

Dev shuffled to the edge.

For a moment nothing happened.

Then his good foot left the concrete.

The rope snapped taut. Leanne’s shoulders jerked, but her feet stayed planted. Dev swung beneath the line with a short, frightened noise that Owen felt in his teeth.

“Got you,” Leanne said, strained now. “Keep your hands where they are.”

Owen clung low on the wall, feet wide, and caught Dev’s shoulder before he struck the jagged floor edge. The weight pulled at his sore wrist. He moved closer rather than trying to hold Dev at arm’s length.

“Pull,” he said.

Leanne hauled in short movements, keeping the rope turned around the steel so they did not lose each gain. Owen guided Dev’s padded leg past the broken tiles. When Dev’s chest reached floor level, Owen stepped down, caught the back of his shirt and helped him crawl clear.

They ended in a heap beside the upright.

Leanne kept hold of the rope until Dev was completely on the floor.

Then she released it and sat down hard.

“Oh,” she said.

Owen looked up.

“My rank’s gone to two.”

[STATUS] Leanne — Slot Rank 2

Her hands were shaking too.

That helped, somehow. Not that she was frightened. That she had done it while frightened, and he had been allowed to see.

They loosened the seat without dragging it across Dev’s wound. With an arm over each of their shoulders, he managed the intact aisles on his good leg. Owen barely noticed the shelves he had been meant to face. At the frontage, Leanne settled Dev on the pavement against the brick pier between the windows, then fetched the first-aid box from behind the tills.

Outside, the streetlights still worked.

Owen found that harder to understand than darkness would have been. The pavement continued past the shop. A parked car sat where a parked car ought to sit. Above the rear roofline rose a cliff that had no business being there.

“Hand,” Leanne said.

She had dressed Dev’s cut. Now she crouched in front of Owen.

“It’s mostly—”

“Which hand?”

He nearly tucked it beneath his other arm. She had seen him cross empty space. He did not want to spoil that by needing help with a wrist.

Then he remembered her shaking hands around the rope.

“This one.”

She asked him to move his fingers and tell her where it hurt. He did. When she told him to rest it rather than keep testing his grip, he stopped.

“You got him out,” she said quietly.

“You pulled.”

“Yes. I did.” She glanced towards the broken roof. “Next time you go somewhere, we agree how you’re coming back.”

He followed her gaze.

Above the torn metal were terraces edged with low handrails. Small trees stood in deliberate rows, their branches pruned clear of narrow paths. A light moved behind them, vanished, then appeared farther along. Another remained beside a dark opening in the cliff.

People. Or something that built paths and carried lights.

His stomach remembered the crossing. Beneath that fear came a pleasure he could not quite suppress: his foot meeting nothing and finding support; his hands catching; a route existing because he could use both.

“There might be help up there,” he said. “Food. Someone who knows what those seeds are.”

Leanne looked at him until he added, “Not yet.”

His arms still trembled. Dev needed more than a dressing. The shop behind them still held supplies they could reach without climbing anything.

But Owen kept watching the moving light.

When he went up, he would have to find a way down that someone else could use.
