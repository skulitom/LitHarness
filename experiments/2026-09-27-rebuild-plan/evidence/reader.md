# reader: Operator reads and product direction: the quality requirements the rebuild must meet

The operator has done 20 reads (2026-08-18 to 09-23). Nothing since has been recorded: there is no reader-read-21.md and no read of the Lite chapter. These reads are the only signal that has ever changed the output, and no book has passed them: "i haven't liked any book yet" (08-28), and at read 20 he "would probably stop reading around overview/beginning".

The defect families have narrowed from architecture (system numbers, cast size, iceberg, hook) to sentence-level families that outlast every prompt clause. Ranked by the number of reads that named each:
- figures that fail a literal read: 10
- trade words, idiom and unexplained terms: 10
- listings with no hook or that read as a list of facts: 8
- narrated inference and manner glosses: 8
- sentence mechanics: 6
- missing or non-diegetic system furniture: 6
- money, debt and institutional framing: named from the first read to the last

What helped and what hurt is unusually clear in the record:
- **Fixed by machinery.** Every family the reads stopped naming was fixed by structure, a deterministic gate or a mechanical rewrite. Those checks made 14 refusals for $7.33 with zero false positives.
- **Clauses failed.** Every family still alive at read 13 had a prompt clause aimed at it.
- **Permission backfires.** Permission-form rules raise what they permit (47.2 against 7.0 number tokens per 1k words, §138).
- **Negations and lists get recited.** "not currency", rent, and a list of six pressures each came back as story material.
- **Big fact packets hurt.** Encyclopedic packets produced minutiae.
- **The agent gate misjudged prose.** It passed material the operator then failed at reads 9-13, 17 and 20.
- **Simulated readers were saturated.** They carried on 4/4 even on shuffled text.

What moved output:
- story-shaped briefs and plan fields (a one-person power that works in chapter 1, a threat, a prize, a look for the system)
- third person
- an exemplar shelf and a narrator stance
- a located rewrite of counter-found tells, which ran at 3-10x the shelf's rate before the rewrite

**For the rebuild:**
- Prompts stay short and structural. Lite's cause/choice/consequence plan fits this.
- Every item the operator can enumerate becomes a deterministic check, with his quotes as test fixtures and a bounded resample on failure.
- LitRPG furniture (a status the character reads, numbers that go up) is kept as minimal plain-text state.
- The operator's read stays the only quality gate.

**Caution.** Lite's win came from a model judge of the same family as the writer. My own spot-check of Lite's chapter found several named families: an institutional premise ("council drainage worker on probation after falsifying a safety inspection"), trade diction, one digit and no rank-up, six named characters, and 4,077 words against a ~1,500-word target.

- [high] (b) HARD PRODUCT CONSTRAINTS the operator stated:
- **Readership.** Male readers aged 20-30 on RoyalRoad. The protagonist is in their twenties, with a prior life the reader has lived (a degree, a job, an obsession), never decades at one craft. He is relatable first and aspirational later.
- **Genre.** LitRPG on every book. 'We shouldn't be writing any books that don't have litrpg as the genre.' The system furniture (a status or interface the character reads) must be present; a progression structure alone does not count.
- **Register.** Popcorn register, and dense prose is a defect on sight. The operator wants flow with varied connectives, not and-chains. He wants implication over statement and an iceberg (the world is bigger than what the page says). He wants plain words with no trade or archaic vocabulary, no em dashes, and no negative-space tell.
- **Person and tense.** Third person, which replaced first person at read 19 (2026-09-02). Past tense is the RoyalRoad default (RR6); the operator never stated it.
- **Chapter length.** About 1,500 words per chapter on RoyalRoad, set 2026-08-20, to be exported at that size 'once a book is good enough'.
- **Hook.** An exception that belongs to one person: they have what nobody else has and progress faster. The power works in chapter 1, and chapter 1 ends with power in hand, not worse off or merely offered something. There is a real threat with a felt scale.
- **Progress.** Felt early, 'as soon as possible', and 'constant and regular'. Numbers go up, and a rank ladder is the number (bronze = 1, gold = 3).
- **Numbers in prose.** Numbers belong only at skill, ability, class or LitRPG-element moments; mundane numbers stay at ordinary-fiction rates. HP/MP/Gold is not the default. Do not list skills at zero. The system is general, never one welded LitRPG system.
- **Diegetic system.** The character opens the status as business in the scene, and deliberating over class or ability options is 'a large part of the story'.
- **Money.** No debt, ledger, licence, court, rent, bills or wages framing anywhere. A cost is paid in the body, in time or in risk.
- **Internal structure stays out.** Schema words never appear in titles or prose. Scenes are internal and never appear in output.
- **Cast.** Early chapters stay small: at read 10, '5 present + 4 offstage names in 1.9k words' was too many.
- **Tone and fun.** Light and fun, not grey (Seth Ring's rule 4).
- **Publishing.** RoyalRoad requires the AI-Generated tag. The chapter body carries no title heading and uses no hard-coded colours.
